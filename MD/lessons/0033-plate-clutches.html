Friction clutches I: single- and multi-plate clutches

About 35 minutes · Bhandari §11.1–11.4

Your win: you can derive the torque capacity of a plate clutch under uniform pressure and under uniform wear, prove that d/D = 0.577 maximises torque for a given outer diameter, and design single- and multi-plate clutches. All three derivations appear regularly in exams.
Warm-up (Lessons 28–29)
Coupling vs clutch?
A coupling is a permanent connection. A clutch connects and disconnects at will.
Friction radius used for a preloaded flange coupling?
(2/3)(Ro³ − Ri³)/(Ro² − Ri²), the uniform pressure version. You'll derive it below.

1. Clutches: types and applications

A clutch connects or disconnects the power source from the rest of the drive at the operator's will. In a clutch, the final state is both members turning together. In a brake, both members end up at rest ().

TypeHow it transmits torqueApplications
Positive (square or spiral jaw, toothed)interlocking jaws: no slip, no heat, but engages only at standstill or near-equal speedpower presses, rolling mills (synchronous drives)
Friction (single plate, multi-plate, cone, centrifugal)friction: smooth engagement, slips on overload (a safety device)automobiles, scooters, machine tools
Electromagneticmagnetic field: fast response, easy controlautomation, servo drives
Fluid couplinghydraulic: very smooth, absorbs shockheavy vehicles, conveyors

Put the clutch on the high-speed side: for the same power, higher speed means lower torque, so a smaller and cheaper clutch ().

2. Derivation: torque capacity of a friction disc

Derive P and Mt for an annular disc (outer D, inner d, friction μ), under (a) uniform pressure and (b) uniform wear (Eqs 11.1–11.8).
  1. Elemental ring of radius r, width dr: area 2πr dr, axial force dP = p(2πr dr), friction μp(2πr dr), friction torque dM = μp(2πr dr)r. So \(P = 2\pi\int p\,r\,dr\) and \(M_t = 2\pi\mu\int p\,r^2\,dr\), with limits d/2 to D/2.
  2. (a) Uniform pressure (new clutch, p constant): \(P = \frac{\pi p}{4}(D^2 - d^2)\), \(M_t = \frac{\pi\mu p}{12}(D^3 - d^3)\). Dividing: \(\boxed{M_t = \dfrac{\mu P}{3}\cdot\dfrac{D^3 - d^3}{D^2 - d^2}}\).
  3. (b) Uniform wear (worn clutch): wear ∝ friction work ∝ (μp)(2πrn) ∝ pr, so uniform wear needs pr = constant = pa(d/2). The maximum pressure pa is at the inner radius.
  4. Then \(P = 2\pi\int p_a\frac{d}{2}\,dr = \frac{\pi p_a d}{2}(D - d)\) and \(M_t = 2\pi\mu p_a\frac{d}{2}\int r\,dr = \frac{\pi\mu p_a d}{8}(D^2 - d^2)\). Dividing: \(\boxed{M_t = \dfrac{\mu P}{4}(D + d)}\).
  5. Both have the form Mt = μPRf. Rf (uniform pressure) is slightly larger than Rf (uniform wear) = (D + d)/4. Most of a lining's life is spent worn in, and the uniform wear torque is the lower (safer) one, so design with uniform wear ().
  6. For z pairs of friction surfaces, multiply by z. A multi-plate clutch with n discs in total has z = n − 1 pairs (the end discs rub on one face only).

3. Derivation: the best inner diameter (Bhandari Ex. 11.4)

Prove that for a fixed outer diameter D and allowable pa, the uniform-wear torque is greatest when d/D = 1/√3 = 0.577.
  1. Put x = d/D: \(M_t = \dfrac{\pi\mu p_a D^3}{8}\,x(1 - x^2)\).
  2. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x - x^3] = 1 - 3x^2 = 0\Rightarrow x = 1/\sqrt3 = 0.577\).
  3. So D/d = 1.732. In practice D/d is taken between 1.5 and 2 ().

4. Single vs multi-plate, dry vs wet, friction materials

  • Multi-plate: more surfaces, so more torque for its size (compact: scooters, motorcycles), but more heat, so usually wet (oil-cooled). Single plate: large radial space (cars, trucks), dry ().
  • Dry vs wet: dry has μ ≥ 0.3 and more torque, but needs sealing from oil and is harder to cool. Wet has μ ≤ 0.1, smoother engagement, and wears at about 1% of the dry rate.
  • Friction material should have high, steady μ over temperature, good thermal conductivity, resistance to moisture and dirt, wear resistance and resilience. Asbestos (woven or moulded) is now banned in many countries (cancer risk). It has been replaced by sintered metal (bronze or iron base) or fibre (steel wool, aramid) + filler + binder (phenol-formaldehyde) + friction modifier. Allowable p: woven 0.3, moulded 1.0, sintered 1–2 N/mm² (–461).

5. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 11.1 (): one pair of surfaces, d = 100, D = 200 mm, μ = 0.2, pa = 1 N/mm², uniform wear. Power at 750 rpm?

P = π(1)(100)(100)/2 = 15 708 N; Mt = 0.2(15 708)(300)/4 = 235 619 N·mm; kW = 18.51. Ex. 11.2 (uniform pressure, same p) gives P = 23 562 N and 28.79 kW: more force and more torque.

Ex. 11.3 (): automotive clutch, 2 pairs of surfaces, 550 N·m, μ = 0.25, pa = 0.5, D = 250 mm, uniform wear. Inner diameter (choose the root giving least lining area)?

Per pair 275 N·m: d(250² − d²) = 5.602 × 10⁶. Roots 174.2 and 112.3 mm. Take 174 mm (less lining). Spring force P = π(0.5)(174)(76)/2 = 10 386 N.

Ex. 11.5 (): 120 N·m engine, (fs) 1.5 against slip, pa = 0.35, μ = 0.35, one pair, designed for maximum torque (d = 0.577D). Inner diameter?

180 000 = π(0.35)(0.35)d(D² − d²)/8 with D² = 3d² gives 2d³ = 3.742 × 10⁶, so d = 123.2 and D = 213.6 mm.

Ex. 11.6 (): 2 pairs, 250 N·m, μ = 0.35, d/D = 175/250, nine springs each 800 N at 5 mm compression. How much lining wear before the clutch slips (uniform wear)?

Safety factors: new 2.16, worn 2.14. Force needed: 125 000 = 0.35P(425)/4, so P = 3361 N, i.e. 373.5 N per spring. Spring rate 160 N/mm: 160(5 − x) = 373.5 ⇒ x = 2.67 mm.

Ex. 11.8 (): multi-disc clutch, 5 steel + 4 bronze plates, d = 75, D = 150, μ = 0.1, pa = 0.3, uniform wear. Power at 750 rpm?

9 plates, so z = 8 pairs. P = π(0.3)(75)(75)/2 = 2650.7 N. Mt = 0.1(2650.7)(8)(225)/4 = 119 282 N·mm, so 9.37 kW.

Ex. 11.9 (): wet multi-disc, 75 N·m, μ = 0.1, pa = 0.5, D = 100, d = 0.577D ≈ 58 mm. Total number of discs?

P = π(0.5)(58)(42)/2 = 1913 N. z = 4(75 000)/(0.1 × 1913 × 158) = 9.92, so 10 pairs and 11 discs (6 plain steel + 5 lined).

6. GATE corner

Under the uniform wear assumption, where is the pressure on a clutch plate greatest?

  1. At the outer radius, where rubbing speed is greatest
  2. At the inner radius, since p × r is constant
  3. Uniformly over the plate, since wear is uniform
  4. At the mean radius of the friction surface
Wear ∝ p·v ∝ p·r. Uniform wear means pr = constant, so p is largest where r is smallest.

For the same axial force and dimensions, which gives the larger torque?

  1. Uniform wear, because pressure is highest inside
  2. Both give the same torque for any d/D
  3. It depends only on the coefficient of friction
  4. Uniform pressure, because its friction radius is larger
Rf = (1/3)(D³ − d³)/(D² − d²) ≥ (D + d)/4. Uniform wear is the conservative design basis.

A multi-plate clutch has 4 discs on the driving shaft and 3 on the driven shaft. The number of pairs of friction surfaces is:

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 12
z = n₁ + n₂ − 1 = 4 + 3 − 1 = 6.

Exam traps

  • In uniform wear, pa is the pressure at the inner radius: P = πpad(D − d)/2.
  • Forgetting the second friction surface on an automotive disc (lining on both faces, so 2 pairs).
  • Counting discs instead of pairs in Mt = μPz(D + d)/4.
