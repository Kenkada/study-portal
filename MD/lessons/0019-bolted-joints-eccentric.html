Unit 2 · Lesson 19

Eccentrically loaded bolted joints

About 30 minutes · Bhandari §7.11–7.13

Your win: you can handle all three of Bhandari's eccentric bolt cases: load in the plane of the bolts (shear, like rivets), load perpendicular to the bolt axes (shear plus tension from tilting), and a circular flange or pillar crane. Each ends with picking the standard M-size.
Warm-up (Lessons 2, 15, 18)
Secondary shear on a rivet in an eccentric group?
P″ = Per/Σr², perpendicular to r from G.
Maximum shear stress with σ and τ together?
\(\tau_{max} = \sqrt{(\sigma/2)^2 + \tau^2}\); principal stress \(\sigma_1 = \sigma/2 + \sqrt{(\sigma/2)^2+\tau^2}\).
Area used for bolt tensile stress?
Core area πdc²/4 or tensile stress area At (Table 7.1).

1. Case 1: load in the plane of the bolts (§7.11)

Identical to rivets (Lesson 15): \(P' = P/n\), \(P''_i = Per_i/\sum r^2\) perpendicular to ri, then vector sum. Size with \(\tau = P_{max}/(\frac{\pi}{4}d_c^2)\) ().

2. Case 2: load perpendicular to the bolt axes (§7.12)

Eqs 7.9–7.14 (two bolts per row) \[ P' = \frac{P}{n}\ \text{(shear)} \qquad P''_1 = \frac{Pe\,l_1}{2(l_1^2 + l_2^2 + \dots)}\ \text{(tension)} \qquad \tau = \frac{P'}{A},\ \ \sigma_t = \frac{P''_1}{A} \] \[ \tau_{max} = \sqrt{(\sigma_t/2)^2 + \tau^2} = \frac{0.5S_{yt}}{(fs)} \quad\text{or}\quad \sigma_1 = \frac{\sigma_t}{2} + \sqrt{(\sigma_t/2)^2+\tau^2} = \frac{S_{yt}}{(fs)} \]

Assumptions: rigid bracket and structure, bolts in reamed holes, no preload, stress concentration ignored. If P is parallel to the bolt axes, add the direct tension P/n to P″ instead of using shear.

3. Case 3: circular base (§7.13)

Flange radius a, bolt circle radius b, n bolts, load P at arm l from the tilting point (Eqs 7.16, 7.18) \[ P_{max} = \frac{2Pl\,(a+b)}{n(2a^2+b^2)}\ \text{(load direction can change: pillar crane)} \] \[ P_{max} = \frac{2Pl\,[a + b\cos(180°/n)]}{n(2a^2+b^2)}\ \text{(fixed direction, two top bolts equally stressed)} \]

Dowel pins usually take the shear, so the bolts carry tension only.

4. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 7.5)

Example 7.5 (). A bracket is held by four bolts, two at l₁ = 150 mm and two at l₂ = 25 mm from the tilting edge. P = 25 kN at e = 100 mm, perpendicular to the bolt axes. 45C8 (Syt = 380), (fs) = 2.5, maximum shear stress theory, no preload. Find the size.
  1. τallow = 0.5(380)/2.5 = 76 N/mm².
  2. Direct shear: P′ = 25 000/4 = 6250 N, so τ = 6250/A.
  3. Tension in bolts 1: \(P''_1 = \dfrac{25\,000(100)(150)}{2(150^2 + 25^2)} = 8108\) N, so σt = 8108/A.
  4. \(\tau_{max} = \frac{1}{A}\sqrt{4054^2 + 6250^2} = \frac{7449.9}{A} = 76 \Rightarrow A = 98.0\) mm².
  5. Table 7.1: M16 (At = 157 mm²). M12 (84.3) is too small.

5. Practice (all Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 7.3 (): four identical bolts at the corners of a 200 mm × 150 mm rectangle (bolts 1–2 are 200 apart, 1–3 are 150 apart). P = 10 kN in the plane of the bolts, e = 500 mm from G. Find the largest resultant shear force on a bolt.

r = √(100² + 75²) = 125 for all four bolts. P′ = 2500 N. P″ = Pe·r/(4r²) = 10 000 × 500/(4 × 125) = 10 000 N. tan θ = 75/100, so θ = 36.87°.

Bolts 2 and 4: \(P = \sqrt{(10\,000\cos36.87° + 2500)^2 + (10\,000\sin36.87°)^2} = 12\,093\) N. With τ = 80 (30C8, fs 2.5): dc = 13.87, so M20.

Ex. 7.4 (): a plate is fixed to a channel by three bolts in a line, 75 mm apart, G at the middle bolt. P = 5 kN at e = 305 mm, in the plane. At the worst bolt P′ and P″ are collinear. Find its force.

P′ = 5000/3 = 1666.7. P″ = 5000(305)(75)/(75² + 75²) = 10 166.7. Bolt 3: 11 833 N. With τ = 63.33 (45C8, fs 3): dc = 15.42, so M20.

Ex. 7.7 (): six bolts in three rows of two, at l₁ = 300, l₂ = 200, l₃ = 100 mm from the tilting edge. P = 50 kN with arm l = 250 mm. Neglect shear; σt ≤ 100 N/mm². Required stress area?

\(P_1 = \frac{50\,000(250)(300)}{2(300^2+200^2+100^2)} = 13\,393\) N. A = 13 393/100 = 133.9 mm², so M16 (157 mm²).

Ex. 7.9 (): a cast-iron bracket with two bolts at l₁ = 50 mm and two at l₂ = 200 mm from the tilting edge. P = 25 kN acts parallel to the bolt axes (tension) at l = 400 mm. σt ≤ 50 N/mm². Required area?

Direct tension 25 000/4 = 6250 N per bolt. Tilting: \(P''_2 = \frac{25\,000(400)(200)}{2(50^2+200^2)} = 23\,529\) N. Total 29 779 N, so A = 595.6 mm², giving M36 (817 mm²).

Ex. 7.11 (): a round flange bearing (flange Ø400) is held by four cap screws on a 300 mm PCD. P = 25 kN at 150 mm from the frame, fixed direction, two top screws equally stressed, dowels take shear. σt ≤ 50 N/mm². Core diameter?

a = 200, b = 150, n = 4: \(P_{max} = \frac{2(25\,000)(150)[200 + 150\cos45°]}{4(2\cdot200^2 + 150^2)} = 5599\) N. dc = √(4 × 5599/(π × 50)) = 11.94 mm, so d ≈ 15 mm (M16).

Ex. 7.12 (): a pillar crane is fixed by 16 bolts on a 2 m PCD; flange Ø2.25 m. 50 kN at 7.5 m radius; σt ≤ 75 N/mm². The crane slews, so the load direction changes. Required area?

Arm about the tilting point l = 7500 − 1125 = 6375 mm. \(P_{max} = \frac{2(50\,000)(6375)(1125+1000)}{16(2\cdot1125^2 + 1000^2)} = 23\,977\) N. A = 319.7 mm², so M24 (353).

In the tilting case, why is bolt tension taken proportional to the distance from the tilting edge?

  1. Bolts farther away have larger diameters
  2. Bolt stretch, so strain and stress, grows with that distance
  3. The external moment is largest at the far bolts
  4. The bracket bends more near the tilting edge
A rigid bracket rotating about C stretches each bolt by δ ∝ l. Then strain ∝ δ, stress ∝ strain, force ∝ stress, so P″ = C·l ().

For a pillar crane, why use \(P_{max} = 2Pl(a+b)/n(2a^2+b^2)\) instead of the equal-stressed-bolts formula?

  1. The pillar crane has an odd number of bolts
  2. Pillar crane bolts carry shear as well as tension
  3. The flange is much larger than the bolt circle
  4. The jib slews, so any bolt can become the worst
When the load direction can change, design for the absolute maximum (a bolt directly opposite the tilting point, α = 180°) ().

Exam traps

  • Taking moments about G for tilting problems. Tilting is about the edge C. Moments about G are only for in-plane eccentricity.
  • Forgetting the "2" in 2(l₁² + l₂²) when there are two bolts per row.
  • Adding direct tension when P is perpendicular to the bolts. Perpendicular load gives direct shear.
