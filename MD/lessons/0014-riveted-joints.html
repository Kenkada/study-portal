Riveted joints: failure modes and efficiency

About 30 minutes · Bhandari §8.19–8.28

Your win: for any lap or butt riveted joint you can write the three strength equations per pitch length, find the weakest one, and give the joint efficiency. You can also design a boiler longitudinal joint. This covers your rivet problem sheet (Q6–Q11) and question bank Q40–Q41.

1. Rivets and joint types

Rivets make permanent joints (like welds; unlike bolts, cotters and splines, which are separable). A rivet has a head, a shank and a point (the closing head). It is specified by shank diameter: 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 48 mm (). Hot riveting (shank at 1000–1100 °C) leaves the shank in tension and clamps the plates, so it is used for fluid-tight joints. Cold riveting is for steel up to 8–10 mm and for non-ferrous rivets.

Where rivets still beat welds (): no heat damage to heat-treated or machined parts; metals with poor weldability (aluminium alloys, aircraft); dissimilar materials (steel to friction lining); vibration and impact; thin sheets.

TypeDescriptionNote
Lap jointPlates overlap; single, double or triple riveted; chain or zig-zag rows.Forces are offset by t, so a bending moment P·t distorts the plates.
Butt jointPlates butt edge to edge; one or two cover straps.Forces in one plane, no bending. Costs more because of the straps.
Diamond (Lozenge)Rivets in diamond pattern, one rivet in the outer row.Bridges and trusses: narrow plates, high efficiency.

Terminology: pitch p ≈ 3d; margin m ≈ 1.5d; transverse (back) pitch pt = 0.8p (chain) or 0.6p (zig-zag); diagonal pitch pd ().

2. How it fails and the strength equations

Five failure modes: shear of rivets, tearing of plate between rivets, crushing of plate (or rivet), shear of plate in the margin, tearing of the margin. With m ≥ 1.5d the last two are avoided, so three equations remain, each per pitch length, with n = rivets per pitch length ():

Strength per pitch length (Eqs 8.35–8.40) \[ P_s = n\,\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2\tau\ \text{(single shear)}\quad P_s = n\,(2\ \text{or}\ 1.875)\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2\tau\ \text{(double shear)} \] \[ P_t = (p-d)\,t\,\sigma_t \qquad P_c = n\,d\,t\,\sigma_c \qquad P = p\,t\,\sigma_t\ \text{(solid plate)} \] \[ \eta = \frac{\min(P_s, P_t, P_c)}{P} \]

Double shear factor: Bhandari and the Indian Boiler Regulations take a rivet in double shear as 1.875× as strong as in single shear. Many textbook problems (and your rivet sheet) use 2×. Use whatever the question states. If it says nothing, write "assuming 2× for double shear".

Caulking and fullering make boiler joints leak-proof: the plate edge is bevelled to 70–75° and hammered with a caulking tool (narrow) or a fullering tool (width equal to plate thickness). Not possible below 6 mm plate ().

3. Worked example: question bank Q40

Q40 (). A double-riveted lap joint joins 15 mm plates; rivet d = 25 mm, p = 75 mm. Ultimate stresses: 400 MPa tension, 320 MPa shear, 640 MPa crushing. Find the minimum force per pitch that ruptures the joint. Then, at (fs) = 4, find the actual stresses.
  1. Tearing: \(P_t = (75-25)(15)(400) = 300\,000\) N.
  2. Shearing (2 rivets per pitch, single shear in a lap joint): \(P_s = 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}(25)^2(320) = 314\,159\) N.
  3. Crushing: \(P_c = 2(25)(15)(640) = 480\,000\) N.
  4. Rupture load = least = 300 kN (tearing). Efficiency = 300 000 / (75·15·400) = 66.7%.
  5. Working load = 300/4 = 75 kN per pitch. Plate tension = 75 000/(50×15) = 100 MPa; rivet shear = 75 000/(2×490.9) = 76.4 MPa; crushing = 75 000/(2×25×15) = 100 MPa.

4. Boiler longitudinal butt joint: the design procedure

Bhandari §8.27 (academic version of the Indian Boiler Regulations)
  1. Shell thickness: \(t = \dfrac{p_iD_i}{2\sigma_t\eta} + CA\) (CA = 1.5–2 mm).
  2. Rivet diameter (t > 8 mm): Unwin's \(d = 6\sqrt t\).
  3. Outer-row pitch from \((p-d)t\sigma_t = (n_1 + 1.875n_2)\frac{\pi}{4}d^2\tau\), limited to \(p_{min}=2d\) and \(p_{max} = Ct + 41.28\) (C from Table 8.7: double-strap butt 1.75, 3.5, 4.63, 5.52, 6.0 for 1–5 rivets per pitch).
  4. Row pitch: outer→next \(p_t = 0.2p + 1.15d\); between full rows \(0.165p + 0.67d\).
  5. Margin \(m = 1.5d\). Straps (equal width): \(t_1 = 0.625t\,\frac{p-d}{p-2d}\); unequal: 0.75t wide, 0.625t narrow.
  6. Check the efficiency.

Typical boiler permissible stresses: σt = 80, τ = 60, σc = 120 N/mm² ().

5. Practice

Rivet sheet Q6: double-riveted lap joint, 16 mm plates, p = 90 mm, d = 25 mm. σt = 140, τ = 110, σc = 240 MPa. Find the efficiency.

Pt = (90−25)(16)(140) = 145 600; Ps = 2(π/4)(625)(110) = 107 992; Pc = 2(25)(16)(240) = 192 000; P = 90(16)(140) = 201 600.

η = 107 992/201 600 = 53.6% (shear governs).

Rivet sheet Q7: single-riveted double-cover butt joint, 10 mm plates, d = 20, p = 60 mm. σt = 100, τ = 80, σc = 160 MPa. Efficiency? (rivets in double shear)

Pt = 40 000; Ps = 2(π/4)(400)(80) = 50 265; Pc = 20(10)(160) = 32 000; P = 60 000. η = 32 000/60 000 = 53.3% (crushing governs, whichever double-shear factor you use).

Rivet sheet Q8: double-riveted double-cover butt joint, 12 mm plates, d = 18, p = 80 mm. σt = 115, τ = 80, σc = 160 MPa. Efficiency?

Pt = (62)(12)(115) = 85 560; Ps = 2×2×(π/4)(324)(80) = 81 430; Pc = 2(18)(12)(160) = 69 120; P = 110 400. η = 62.6% (crushing).

Question bank Q41(2): double-riveted lap joint, 6 mm plates, d = 20 mm, p = 65 mm. σt = 120, τ = 90, σc = 180 MPa. Efficiency? (Part 1, the single-riveted version with p = 50, gives 60%.)

Pt = (45)(6)(120) = 32 400; Ps = 2(π/4)(400)(90) = 56 549; Pc = 2(20)(6)(180) = 43 200; P = 65(6)(120) = 46 800. η = 69.2% (tearing).

Rivet sheet Q9: design a double-riveted chain lap joint for 10 mm plates (σt = 60, τ = 50, σc = 80 MPa). Take d = 6√t rounded to 19 mm. Find the pitch by equating tearing and shearing strength.

(p − 19)(10)(60) = 2(π/4)(19²)(50) ⇒ p = 66.25, so 66 mm (pmax = 2.62(10) + 41.28 = 67.5 ✓). Back pitch (chain) ≈ 2d = 38 mm.

Efficiency: Pt = 28 200, Ps = 28 353, Pc = 30 400, P = 39 600, so η = 71.2%.

Bhandari Ex. 8.22 (): boiler, Di = 1 m, pi = 2.5 MPa, double-riveted double-strap butt joint (equal straps, 3 rivets per pitch, all in double shear), η ≥ 70%, CA = 2 mm, σt = 80, τ = 60. Taking t = 25, d = 30, p = 150 mm, find the joint efficiency.

t = 2.5(1000)/(2·80·0.7) + 2 = 24.3 → 25 mm; d = 6√25 = 30; p = 3(1.875)(π/4)(900)(60)/(25·80) + 30 = 149.3 → 150 (≤ pmax = 157).

Pt = (120)(25)(80) = 240 000; Ps = 5.625(π/4)(900)(60) = 238 565; P = 300 000. η = 79.5%.

What is the main advantage of a butt joint over a lap joint?

  1. It needs fewer rivets for the same load
  2. There is no bending from offset forces
  3. It is cheaper because no straps are needed
  4. It always gives higher crushing strength
In a lap joint the two forces are offset by t, which bends and warps the plates. A butt joint keeps the forces in one plane (). Its drawback is the cost of the straps.

Which failure modes are avoided by giving a margin of at least 1.5d?

  1. Shearing and crushing of the rivets
  2. Tearing of the plate between rivets
  3. Bending of the plates in a lap joint
  4. Shearing and tearing of the plate margin
A margin of 1.5d gives enough plate in front of the hole to prevent the margin shearing or tearing out, so only the three main equations need checking.

Why is the longitudinal joint of a boiler a butt joint, while the circumferential joint can be a lap joint?

  1. Hoop stress is twice the longitudinal stress
  2. Butt joints are easier to make leak-proof
  3. The circumferential joint carries no stress
  4. Indian Boiler Regulations forbid lap joints
The longitudinal seam carries the hoop (circumferential) stress, which is twice the longitudinal stress, so it needs the stronger, more efficient joint ().

Exam traps

  • Lap-joint rivets are in single shear. Double-strap butt-joint rivets are in double shear.
  • n is rivets per pitch length, not the total in the joint.
  • Efficiency compares with the solid plate of width p, not the whole plate.
  • In boiler design, cap the pitch at pmax = Ct + 41.28 (Ex. 8.21: 230 mm became 160 mm).
