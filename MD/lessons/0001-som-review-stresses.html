Unit 1 · Lesson 1

Strength of materials review: direct, bending and torsional stress

About 20 minutes · Bhandari §4.2–4.8

Your win: given a part, its loads and a material, you can find the allowable stress and every stress the loads cause. That covers direct, shear, bending, torsion and eccentric loading. Every later lesson is built from these five.

Every design problem in this course has three parts: allowable stress from the material, induced stress from the loads, and setting one equal to the other to get a size. This lesson is the toolkit for the middle part. Your question bank starts with exactly these problems (hollow shaft under thrust, bending and torque; eccentrically loaded column).

1. Allowable stress comes from the material, never from the question

Bhandari is firm about this (): you derive the allowable stress from a strength and a factor of safety (fs).

Allowable stresses (Eqs 4.1, 4.2, 4.11) \[ \sigma = \frac{S_{yt}}{(fs)}\ \text{(ductile)} \qquad \sigma = \frac{S_{ut}}{(fs)}\ \text{(brittle, e.g. cast iron)} \qquad \tau = \frac{S_{sy}}{(fs)},\ \ S_{sy}=0.5\,S_{yt} \]

Units: 1 MPa = 1 N/mm². Bhandari works in N, mm and N/mm² throughout, so torque goes in N·mm. Convert N·m to N·mm (× 1000) before substituting.

A material code tells you the strength. FG 200 is grey cast iron with \(S_{ut}=200\) N/mm² (brittle, so use \(S_{ut}\)). 30C8 is plain carbon steel (ductile, so use \(S_{yt}\), which the question usually gives).

Torque from power, and angle of twist (Eqs 4.18, 4.21, 4.23) \[ kW = \frac{2\pi n M_t}{60\times10^6}\ \ (M_t\ \text{in N·mm},\ n\ \text{in rpm}) \qquad \theta = \frac{M_t\, l}{J G}\ \text{rad} = \frac{584\, M_t\, l}{G d^4}\ \text{deg} \]

Steel: E = 207 000 N/mm², G = 80 000 N/mm², μ = 0.29. Grey cast iron: E = 100 000, G = 40 000 (–81).

3. Worked example: three loads on one hollow shaft

Question bank Q1 (). Hollow shaft, \(d_o = 40\) mm, \(d_i = 25\) mm. Torque 120 N·m, axial thrust 10 kN and bending moment 80 N·m all act together. Find the maximum compressive stress and the maximum shear stress.
  1. Section properties. \(A = \frac{\pi}{4}(40^2-25^2) = 765.8\) mm², \(I = \frac{\pi}{64}(40^4-25^4) = 106\,489\) mm⁴, \(J = 2I = 212\,978\) mm⁴.
  2. Direct stress from the thrust (compressive). \(\sigma_d = 10\,000/765.8 = 13.06\) N/mm².
  3. Bending stress at the outer fibre. \(\sigma_b = M_b y/I = 80\,000 \times 20 / 106\,489 = 15.03\) N/mm². On the side that is compressed by bending, it adds to the thrust stress.
  4. Maximum compressive stress (normal stress on the cross-section): \(\sigma_x = -(13.06 + 15.03) = -28.08\) N/mm².
  5. Torsional shear stress. \(\tau = M_t r / J = 120\,000 \times 20/212\,978 = 11.27\) N/mm².
  6. Combine (preview of Lesson 2). Principal stresses: \(\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_x}{2} \pm \sqrt{(\sigma_x/2)^2 + \tau^2} = -14.04 \pm 18.00\). So \(\sigma_2 = -32.05\) N/mm² is the largest compressive stress at the point, and \(\tau_{max} = 18.00\) N/mm².

The question means the last step. Reporting only −28.08 and 11.27 loses marks, because the combined state is worse than either stress alone.

4. Practice

A solid steel shaft transmits 10 kW at 720 rpm. \(S_{yt} = 380\) N/mm², (fs) = 2, \(S_{sy} = 0.5S_{yt}\). Ignore bending. Find the minimum diameter.

\(M_t = \dfrac{10 \times 60\times10^6}{2\pi(720)} = 132\,629\) N·mm. Allowable \(\tau = 0.5(380)/2 = 95\) N/mm².

\(95 = \dfrac{16(132\,629)}{\pi d^3}\Rightarrow d = 19.23\) mm, so choose 20 mm.

Bhandari Example 4.1: two plates carrying 50 kN are joined by three rivets in single shear. Material 10C4, \(S_{yt}=250\) N/mm², \(S_{sy} = 0.5S_{yt}\), (fs) = 2.5. Find the rivet diameter (before rounding up).

\(\tau = 0.5(250)/2.5 = 50\) N/mm². \(3 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}d^2 \cdot 50 = 50\,000 \Rightarrow d = 20.60\) mm, so use 22 mm ().

Question bank Q10 (): a hollow column (\(d_o = 190\) mm, \(d_i = 0.8 d_o\)) carries a bracket load P whose line of action is 500 mm from the column axis. Steel FeE 200 (\(S_{yt}=200\)), (fs) = 4, and only maximum tensile stress counts. Find the largest P.

\(A = 10\,207\) mm², \(I = 37.77\times10^6\) mm⁴, allowable \(\sigma_t = 50\) N/mm².

The load compresses the column directly (−P/A), while bending puts the far fibre in tension (+P·e·y/I). Net tension: \(P\left(-\frac{1}{10\,207} + \frac{500 \times 95}{37.77\times10^6}\right) = 50\Rightarrow P = 43\,115\) N ≈ 43.1 kN.

A 40 mm solid steel shaft, 1 m long, carries 500 N·m of torque. G = 80 000 N/mm². What is the angle of twist?

\(\theta = \dfrac{584 M_t l}{G d^4} = \dfrac{584(500\,000)(1000)}{80\,000(40^4)} = 1.43°\).

A bracket is made of grey cast iron FG 200, with (fs) = 3.5. What allowable tensile stress do you design with?

  1. 200 / 3.5 × 0.5 = 28.6 N/mm²
  2. Yield strength / 3.5, from tables
  3. 200 / 3.5 = 57.1 N/mm²
  4. 200 × 3.5 = 700 N/mm²
Cast iron is brittle: it fractures without yielding, so the ultimate strength \(S_{ut}\) = 200 N/mm² (the number in FG 200) divided by (fs) gives the allowable stress (Eq. 4.2). This is Bhandari Example 4.12.

The diameter of a solid shaft is doubled and the torque stays the same. What happens to the maximum torsional shear stress?

  1. It halves
  2. It drops to one-eighth
  3. It drops to one-quarter
  4. It stays the same
\(\tau = 16M_t/\pi d^3\), so stress varies as \(1/d^3\). Doubling d divides the stress by 8. This is why shaft diameters come out as cube roots.

For a rectangular beam section of width b and depth d, bent about the axis parallel to b, which expression is I?

  1. \(db^3/12\)
  2. \(bd^2/6\)
  3. \(\pi bd^3/64\)
  4. \(bd^3/12\)
The dimension perpendicular to the neutral axis gets cubed (Eq. 4.13). \(bd^2/6\) is the section modulus Z = I/y, not I.

Exam traps

  • Mixing N·m and N·mm. A shaft 10× too thin is the usual result.
  • Using \(S_{yt}\) for cast iron. Brittle parts are designed on \(S_{ut}\).
  • Forgetting that direct and bending stresses add on one side and subtract on the other. Find the side where they add.
  • Rounding too early. Keep four significant figures until the final size, then round up to a standard size.
