Helical gears

About 40 minutes · Bhandari Ch. 18

Your win: you can relate normal and transverse pitch, module and pressure angle, and derive the virtual number of teeth z/cos³ψ and the minimum face width. You can find all three force components with the right thrust direction, and turn the spur-gear Lewis and Buckingham equations into their helical forms to design or rate a helical pair.
Warm-up (Lessons 43–44)
Spur beam strength and wear strength?
Sb = mbσbY; Sw = bQd′pK with Q = 2zg/(zg + zp).
Why are spur gears noisy at high speed?
Contact begins suddenly across the whole face width, an impact at every tooth.
Velocity factor for precision gears?
Cv = 5.6/(5.6 + √v).

1. Why helical, and the two kinds

Helical teeth are cut along a helix on the pitch cylinder. Contact starts at a point on the leading edge and spreads diagonally across the tooth, so the load is picked up gradually. That gives smooth, quiet running at high speed (cars, turbines, up to 3000 m/min). Parallel helical gears have the same helix angle and opposite hands, a right-hand pinion meshing with a left-hand gear. Crossed helical gears sit on non-parallel shafts (). The price is an axial thrust on the bearings, and the cure for that is herringbone gearing.

2. Derivation: normal and transverse geometry (§18.2, 18.4)

Derive pn = p cos ψ, mn = m cos ψ, d = zmn/cos ψ, a = mn(z₁ + z₂)/2cos ψ and bmin = πmn/sin ψ.
  1. In the pitch plane, adjacent tooth centre-lines make the helix angle ψ with the axis. The transverse pitch p is measured in the plane of rotation and the normal pitch pn perpendicular to the teeth. From the right triangle between them: \(p_n = p\cos\psi\). Dividing by π: \(m_n = m\cos\psi\). Cutters are made to the normal module, so mn is the standard value (1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10).
  2. Axial pitch \(p_a = p/\tan\psi\). The pressure angles are related by \(\tan\alpha_n = \tan\alpha\cos\psi\), where αn = 20° (normal) is standard, so the transverse α is larger.
  3. Pitch diameter \(d = zm = \dfrac{zm_n}{\cos\psi}\), and centre distance \(a = \dfrac{m_n(z_1 + z_2)}{2\cos\psi}\). A centre distance can be met exactly by adjusting ψ. Addendum mn and dedendum 1.25mn, so \(d_a = m_n\big(\tfrac{z}{\cos\psi} + 2\big)\) and \(d_f = m_n\big(\tfrac{z}{\cos\psi} - 2.5\big)\).
  4. Minimum face width: for at least one point of contact at every instant, the leading edge must be ahead of the trailing edge by more than one circular pitch: b tan ψ ≥ p. So \(b \ge \dfrac{p}{\tan\psi} = \dfrac{\pi m_n}{\cos\psi\tan\psi} = \boxed{\dfrac{\pi m_n}{\sin\psi}}\) (Eq. 18.14). In practice ψ is 15°–25° (–697).

3. Derivation: the virtual (formative) number of teeth (§18.3)

Derive \(z' = \dfrac{z}{\cos^3\psi}\).
  1. Cut the pitch cylinder with a plane normal to the teeth. The section is an ellipse with semi-major axis a = d/(2 cos ψ) and semi-minor axis b = d/2.
  2. The teeth near the pitch point lie on a curve whose radius is the ellipse's radius of curvature at the end of the minor axis: \(r' = \dfrac{a^2}{b} = \dfrac{d}{2\cos^2\psi}\).
  3. Imagine a spur gear of radius r′ and module mn in that plane: the formative gear. Its teeth: \(z' = \dfrac{2\pi r'}{p_n} = \dfrac{\pi d/\cos^2\psi}{\pi m_n} = \dfrac{d}{m_n\cos^2\psi}\).
  4. With d = zmn/cos ψ: \(\boxed{z' = \dfrac{z}{\cos^3\psi}}\). The tooth strength is that of this larger virtual spur gear, so read the Lewis Y at z′ (interpolating in Table 17.3) (–696).

4. Derivation: force components (§18.5)

Components (Eqs 18.15–18.17) \[ P_t = \frac{2M_t}{d},\qquad P_a = P_t\tan\psi,\qquad P_r = P_t\,\frac{\tan\alpha_n}{\cos\psi} \]

Derivation: Pr = P sin αn, Pt = P cos αn cos ψ and Pa = P cos αn sin ψ. Divide to eliminate P.

Thrust direction (–699). Take the driving gear. Use your right hand for a right-hand helix and your left hand for a left-hand helix. Curl the fingers in its direction of rotation: the thumb points along the thrust on the driver. The driven gear's thrust is opposite. Pt and Pr follow the spur-gear rules.

5. Derivation: beam and wear strength (§18.6–18.8)

Derive Sb = mnbσbY and \(S_w = \dfrac{bQd_pK}{\cos^2\psi}\) from the formative spur gear.
  1. The formative gear lies in the plane normal to the teeth. Its module is mn, its face width along the tooth is b/cos ψ, and Y is read at z′. Lewis in that plane: \((S_b)_n = m_n\dfrac{b}{\cos\psi}\sigma_bY\).
  2. The useful (tangential) component in the plane of rotation is (Sb)n cos ψ, so \(\boxed{S_b = m_nb\sigma_bY}\) (Eq. 18.18), the same form as for spur gears, with mn and Y(z′).
  3. Wear: the formative pinion has diameter dp/cos²ψ and face b/cos ψ. Buckingham gives \((S_w)_n = \dfrac{b}{\cos\psi}\,Q\,\dfrac{d_p}{\cos^2\psi}\,K\). Its tangential component is (Sw)n cos ψ: \(\boxed{S_w = \dfrac{bQd_pK}{\cos^2\psi}}\) (Eq. 18.24).
  4. Q from the virtual teeth reduces to the actual ones, Q = 2zg/(zg + zp) (or zg − zp for internal gears). K = 0.16(BHN/100)² for steel at αn = 20°.
Effective load (Eqs 18.19–18.22) \[ P_{eff} = \frac{C_sP_t}{C_v},\ \ C_v = \frac{5.6}{5.6 + \sqrt v}\qquad\text{or}\qquad P_{eff} = C_sP_t + \frac{21v\,(Ceb\cos^2\psi + P_t)\cos\psi}{21v + \sqrt{Ceb\cos^2\psi + P_t}} \]

6. Herringbone and crossed helical gears (§18.9–18.10)

  • Herringbone / double helical: two helical gears of opposite hand on one blank. Their thrusts cancel, so they carry high power at high speed, with ψ = 20°–45° (no thrust limit). They are costly, and the two halves must share the load equally, so one shaft is left free to float axially. A double helical gear has a groove between the halves (cut by a hob). A herringbone has none (cut by two reciprocating cutters). Design each half as a helical gear carrying half the power. Used in ship drives and turbines (–707).
  • Crossed helical: point contact, so a low load capacity, a lot of sliding and poor efficiency. Used for speedometer and oil-pump drives and machine-tool feeds. Shaft angle Σ = ψ₁ + ψ₂ (same hand) or ψ₁ − ψ₂ (opposite hand). At 90°, both are 45° and of the same hand. Speed ratio \(i = \dfrac{z_g}{z_p} = \dfrac{d_g\cos\psi_g}{d_p\cos\psi_p}\) (–709).

7. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 18.4)

Ex. 18.4 (): 20-tooth pinion at 720 rpm, 100-tooth gear, αn = 20°, ψ = 25°, b = 40, mn = 4, steel 40C8 (Sut = 600) at 300 BHN, Cs = 1.5, (fs) = 2, velocity factor. Power capacity?
  1. Pinion weaker. z′ = 20/cos³25° = 26.87, so Y = 0.344 + 0.004(0.87) = 0.3475. Sb = 4(40)(200)(0.3475) = 11 120 N.
  2. dp = 20(4)/cos 25° = 88.27 mm. Q = 200/120 = 1.667 and K = 0.16(3)² = 1.44. Sw = 40(1.667)(88.27)(1.44)/cos²25° = 10 318.6 N. Wear governs (it is lower).
  3. v = π(88.27)(720)/60 000 = 3.328 m/s, so Cv = 5.6/(5.6 + √3.328) = 0.7543.
  4. Sw = Peff(fs): 10 318.6 = (1.5Pt/0.7543)(2), so Pt = 2594.4 N.
  5. Mt = 2594.4 × 88.27/2 = 114 505 N·mm, so kW = 2π(720)(114 505)/(60 × 10⁶) = 8.63 kW.

9. GATE corner

A helical gear has 20 teeth and a helix angle of 25°. Its virtual (formative) number of teeth is about:

  1. 22.1
  2. 24.4
  3. 26.9
  4. 29.7
z′ = z/cos³ψ = 20/0.7444 = 26.9. The distractors are z/cos ψ, z/cos²ψ and z/cos⁴ψ.

For a helical gear with tangential force Pt and helix angle ψ, the axial thrust is:

  1. Pt tan ψ
  2. Pt sin ψ
  3. Pt cos ψ
  4. Pt sec ψ
Pa = P cos αn sin ψ and Pt = P cos αn cos ψ, so Pa/Pt = tan ψ.

The main reason for using herringbone gears instead of single helical gears is to:

  1. allow a smaller helix angle on each half
  2. reduce the cost of cutting the gear teeth
  3. make the gear pair work on crossed shafts
  4. cancel the axial thrust on the shaft bearings
Opposite helices give equal and opposite thrusts inside the gear, and that allows ψ up to 45° (§18.9).

For a helical gear with normal pressure angle 20°, the transverse pressure angle is:

  1. less than 20°, by the factor cos ψ
  2. more than 20°, since tan α = tan αn/cos ψ
  3. exactly 20°, as both planes see one profile
  4. equal to the helix angle of the teeth
tan αn = tan α cos ψ. Ex. 18.1 gives α = 21.88° for ψ = 25°.

Exam traps

  • The standard module is the normal module mn. Pitch diameter = zmn/cos ψ, not zmn.
  • Read Y at z′ = z/cos³ψ and interpolate. Do not use the actual z.
  • Helical wear strength has cos²ψ in the denominator.
  • For a herringbone gear, put half the power into each helical half. Do not double the face width with full power on one half.
  • Pr = Pt tan αn/cos ψ. Writing Pt tan αn forgets the 1/cos ψ.
