Unit 1 · Lesson 2

Principal stresses and theories of failure

About 25 minutes · Bhandari §4.13–4.18

Your win: when a part carries bending and torsion (or tension and shear) together, you can pick the right failure theory and turn the combined stresses into a single diameter.
Warm-up: recall from Lesson 1 (answer before opening)
Bending stress at the surface of a solid round bar?
\(\sigma_b = 32M_b/\pi d^3\)
Allowable stress for a cast-iron part?
\(S_{ut}/(fs)\). Cast iron is brittle, so it fails by fracture, not yielding.

1. Principal stresses: the worst normal and shear stress at a point

A shaft surface element carries a normal stress \(\sigma_x\) (from bending or axial load) and a shear stress \(\tau_{xy}\) (from torque) at the same time. Rotate the element and, at one angle, the shear vanishes. The normal stresses there are the principal stresses ():

Principal stresses and principal shear stress (Eqs 4.31, 4.32, 4.34) \[ \sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2} \qquad \tau_{max} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2} \]

For a shaft, \(\sigma_y = 0\). Substitute \(\sigma = 32M_b/\pi d^3\) and \(\tau = 16M_t/\pi d^3\) and two very useful shortcuts drop out:

Shaft shortcuts (bending \(M_b\) + torque \(M_t\)) \[ \sigma_1 = \frac{16}{\pi d^3}\left(M_b + \sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2}\right) \qquad \tau_{max} = \frac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2} \]
Principal stress calculator (try σx = 80, σy = −40, τ = 30)

2. Three theories, one question: when does the combination fail?

A tension test gives you \(S_{yt}\) for a bar under pure tension. A real part has \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\) and shear at once. A theory of failure links the two ().

TheoryFailure happens when…Design equationUse for
Maximum principal stress (Rankine)\(\sigma_1\) reaches the strength\(\sigma_1 = \dfrac{S_{ut}}{(fs)}\) or \(\dfrac{S_{yt}}{(fs)}\)brittle materials (cast iron)
Maximum shear stress (Tresca, Guest)\(\tau_{max}\) reaches the shear stress at yield in the tension test, \(S_{yt}/2\)\(\tau_{max} = \dfrac{0.5\,S_{yt}}{(fs)}\)ductile, quick and conservative
Distortion energy (von Mises, Hencky)distortion strain energy reaches its value at yield in the tension test\(\sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2} = \dfrac{S_{yt}}{(fs)}\)ductile, most accurate

Shear yield strength: 0.5 Syt under maximum shear stress theory, 0.577 Syt under distortion energy (, ). Use the one that matches the theory the question names.

For the common case of one normal stress σ plus shear τ (a shaft, or a bolt in tension plus shear), distortion energy simplifies to \(\sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2} = S_{yt}/(fs)\). For a shaft that becomes:

Distortion energy for a shaft \[ \frac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{4M_b^2 + 3M_t^2} = \frac{S_{yt}}{(fs)} \]

Choosing, in one line: brittle → maximum principal stress. Ductile → maximum shear stress (easy, conservative) or distortion energy (accurate). If the question names a theory, use that one. If it doesn't, say which you chose and why. That sentence earns a mark.

3. Worked example: overhang crank (Bhandari Ex. 4.13)

Example 4.13 (). A 1 kN force acts on the crankpin of an overhang crank. At the critical section A of the shaft, the bending arm is 250 mm and the torque arm is 500 mm. Steel 45C8, \(S_{yt}=380\) N/mm², (fs) = 2. Find d by the maximum shear stress theory.
  1. Allowable shear stress. \(S_{sy}=0.5(380)=190\) N/mm², so \(\tau_{max}=190/2=95\) N/mm².
  2. Moments at A. \(M_b = 1000\times250 = 250\times10^3\) N·mm and \(M_t = 1000\times500 = 500\times10^3\) N·mm.
  3. Maximum shear stress at A. \(\tau_{max} = \dfrac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2} = \dfrac{16\sqrt{(250^2+500^2)}\times10^3}{\pi d^3} = \dfrac{2847.05\times10^3}{d^3}\)
  4. Size. \(2847.05\times10^3/d^3 = 95 \Rightarrow d = 31.06\) mm, so choose 32 mm.

4. Practice

Question bank Q4 ()A solid shaft of steel 50C8 (\(S_{yt} = 700\) N/mm²) carries a bending moment of 10 000 N·m and a twisting moment of 30 000 N·m. (fs) = 2. Find the diameter by each theory.

(a) Maximum principal stress theory.

\(\dfrac{16}{\pi d^3}\left(M_b+\sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2}\right) = \dfrac{700}{2}\). With \(M_b=10\times10^6\) and \(M_t=30\times10^6\) N·mm: \(d = 84.6\) mm. (Your professor's answer: 84.59 mm.)

(b) Maximum shear stress theory.

\(\dfrac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2} = \dfrac{0.5(700)}{2} = 175\Rightarrow d = 97.27\) mm.

(c) Distortion energy theory.

\(\dfrac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{4M_b^2+3M_t^2} = 350\Rightarrow d = 93.22\) mm. The order is MPST < DET < MSST: maximum shear stress is the most conservative of the two ductile theories.

Question bank Q3 (): a bolt carries an axial pull of 10 kN and a transverse shear force of 5 kN. \(S_{yt}=400\) N/mm², (fs) = 4. Use the distortion energy theory to find the diameter of the stressed section.

\(\sigma = 10\,000/A\), \(\tau = 5000/A\). \(\sqrt{\sigma^2+3\tau^2} = 400/4 = 100\).

\(\frac{1}{A}\sqrt{10\,000^2 + 3(5000)^2} = 100\Rightarrow A = 132.3\) mm² \(\Rightarrow d = \sqrt{4A/\pi} = 12.98\) mm. (Maximum shear stress theory would give 13.42 mm.)

A grey cast-iron bracket carries combined bending and direct tension. Which theory should you use?

  1. Maximum principal stress theory, because cast iron fractures
  2. Maximum shear stress theory, because it is the most conservative
  3. Distortion energy theory, because it is the most accurate
  4. Any theory, because they agree for every material
Brittle materials fail by fracture when the largest normal stress reaches \(S_{ut}\). Their compressive strength is also much higher than their tensile strength, which only the principal stress theory allows for (, point vii).

For a ductile part in pure shear, what yield strength in shear does the distortion energy theory predict?

  1. 0.500 Syt
  2. 0.707 Syt
  3. 0.577 Syt
  4. 1.000 Syt
In pure shear, \(\sigma_1 = -\sigma_2 = \tau\). Substituting into \(\sigma_1^2-\sigma_1\sigma_2+\sigma_2^2 = S_{yt}^2\) gives \(3\tau^2 = S_{yt}^2\), so \(\tau = S_{yt}/\sqrt3 = 0.577S_{yt}\) (Eq. 4.45). The 0.5 value belongs to the maximum shear stress theory.

Why is the maximum principal stress theory dangerous for a ductile shaft in torsion?

  1. It ignores the bending moment completely
  2. It predicts safety outside the true boundary
  3. It always gives a far larger diameter
  4. It needs the compressive strength too
In the 2nd and 4th quadrants (stresses of opposite sign, as in torsion), the square sits outside the distortion energy ellipse. A point can be inside the square, so "safe", while actually past yield.

Exam traps

  • Writing \(\tau_{max} = S_{yt}/(fs)\). Under maximum shear stress theory it is \(0.5S_{yt}/(fs)\).
  • Using \(\sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2}\) in the principal stress theory. That theory needs \(M_b + \sqrt{M_b^2+M_t^2}\).
  • Not stating the theory. Write "Using maximum shear stress theory (ductile material)…" as the first line.
