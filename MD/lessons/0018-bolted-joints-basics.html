What property lets a part absorb shock elastically?

1. Kinds of threaded fastening

FastenerWhat it isUse when (–222)
Through boltHead + shank + thread; turned into a nutMedium-thickness parts with room for head, nut and spanner; parts too weak for threads; frequent dismantling
Tap bolt / cap screwTurned into a tapped hole in one partOne part thick and strong enough for threads, no room for a nut, rarely dismantled (tapped threads wear)
StudRod threaded at both ends: one end in a tapped hole, nut on the otherLike a tap bolt but dismantled often, e.g. cylinder head to cylinder
SetscrewPoint presses on the other part; compression onlyStop relative motion (light duty, instead of a key). Points: flat, dog, cone, hanger, cut

Advantages of threaded joints: high clamping force from the wedge action, small spanner effort, compact, self-locking, cheap, detachable, standardised. Disadvantages: holes cause stress concentration, they loosen under vibration, and assembly is costly (DFMA says use few fasteners) ().

2. Thread terminology and ISO metric threads

Major (nominal) diameter d; minor (core/root) dc; pitch diameter dp (thread width = space width); pitch p; lead (axial travel per turn); thread angle 60°. Tensile stress area = area of a rod of diameter (dp + dc)/2, which has the same strength as the threaded rod ().

Coarse threads: higher static strength, easier to cut, less affected by wear, less seizing. Use for general work. Fine threads: better under fluctuating load, resist self-loosening (smaller helix angle). Use for vibration (automobiles), thin walls and adjustment. Designation: M12 (coarse), M12 × 1.25 (fine).

Rolled vs cut threads (): rolling leaves compressive residual stress, rounded roots, unbroken grain flow and no waste. Better fatigue strength.

3. Locking devices and bolts of uniform strength

  • Locking (): extra friction (jam/lock nut, split nut, spring washer, setscrew with soft pad) or positive locking (castle nut + split pin). Threads loosen under vibration and cyclic load even though they are self-locking.
  • Bolt of uniform strength: for shock loads, make the shank as highly stressed as the threads so it absorbs more energy. Either turn the shank down to dc, or drill an axial hole of \(d_1 = \sqrt{d^2 - d_c^2}\). Turning down is preferred. A longer shank also helps ().

4. Simple analysis

Eqs 7.2–7.4, 7.19 \[ \sigma_t = \frac{P}{\frac{\pi}{4}d_c^2}\ \ (\text{or } P/A_t) \qquad d_c \approx 0.8d \qquad \text{nut height } h = 0.8d \qquad M_t \approx 0.2\,P_i\,d \]

Equal strength of thread shear and core tension needs only h = 0.5dc ≈ 0.4d, so a standard 0.8d nut never strips first. Bolt in shear (fitted bolt): \(\tau = P/(n\frac{\pi}{4}d^2)\) on the shank.

5. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 7.1)

Example 7.1 (). An eye bolt lifts a 10 kN motor. Coarse threads, 30C8 (Syt = 400), (fs) = 6. Find the size.
  1. σt = 400/6 = 66.67 N/mm².
  2. \(66.67 = 10\,000/(\frac{\pi}{4}d_c^2) \Rightarrow d_c = 13.82\) mm.
  3. d ≈ dc/0.8 = 17.3 mm. From the table, M16 has dc = 13.546 (too small), so choose M20. (Check with At: 10 000/66.67 = 150 mm² needed; M16 has 157, which some examiners accept. State your basis.)

6. Practice

Ex. 7.2: two plates are joined by two fitted bolts in single shear carrying 5 kN. 30C8 (Syt = 400), (fs) = 5, Ssy = 0.5Syt. Shank diameter?

τ = 200/5 = 40. 5000 = 2(π/4)d²(40) ⇒ d = 8.92, so M10.

Formula recall (no calculation needed)
Wrench torque to give preload Pi in a bolt of nominal diameter d?
Mt ≈ 0.2 Pi d (Eq. 7.19, μ ≈ 0.15). For example, 10 kN in an M12 bolt needs about 24 N·m.
Axial hole that turns an ordinary bolt into a bolt of uniform strength?
\(d_1 = \sqrt{d^2 - d_c^2}\) (Eq. 7.1), which makes the shank area equal to the core area.

A cast-iron cylinder needs a head that is removed often for maintenance. Which fastener?

  1. Through bolts, since cast iron is brittle
  2. Tap bolts screwed into the cast iron
  3. Studs fixed in the block, with nuts on top
  4. Setscrews pressing the head onto the block
Repeatedly screwing tap bolts in and out would wear the tapped threads in the cast iron. Studs stay put in the block and only the nuts come off ().

Why do fine threads resist loosening better than coarse threads?

  1. Their smaller helix angle gives more self-locking
  2. Their larger core diameter adds friction
  3. Their tensile stress area is always larger
  4. They are rolled while coarse ones are cut
A smaller pitch means a smaller helix angle, well below the friction angle, so they resist self-unscrewing under vibration ().

Which locking device gives positive locking, not just extra friction?

  1. A jam nut tightened against the main nut
  2. A castle nut with a split pin through the bolt
  3. A spring washer biting the nut and base
  4. A split nut opened by a small cap screw
The split pin passes through the castle nut's slot and a hole in the bolt, so the nut cannot turn unless the pin shears. The others rely on friction.

Exam traps

  • Designing on the nominal diameter in tension. Use dc or At.
  • Rounding a calculated d to a non-standard size. Pick from the M-series table.
  • Confusing pitch and lead (they differ for multi-start threads; see Lesson 22).
