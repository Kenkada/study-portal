Bevel gears

About 40 minutes · Bhandari Ch. 19

Your win: you can name the bevel gear types and terms, and use Tredgold's approximation to get the formative number of teeth z/cos γ. You can find the three tooth-force components at the mean radius and derive the Lewis equation for bevel gears, including the bevel factor (1 − b/A₀), by integrating along the face. Then you can rate a bevel pair for bending and pitting.
Warm-up (Lessons 43–45)
Formative teeth of a helical gear, and why we need them?
z′ = z/cos³ψ. The tooth strength is that of the equivalent spur gear in the normal plane, so Y is read at z′.
Ratio factor Q for external spur gears?
Q = 2zg/(zg + zp).
Separating (radial) force on a spur gear?

1. Types and uses (§19.1)

Bevel gears transmit power between intersecting shafts, almost always at 90° (–713).

  • Straight bevel: simple to design and make, noisy at high speed. Spiral bevel: smooth, quiet and stronger, but costly. The comparison is the same as spur against helical.
  • By pitch angle: external (under 90°), crown (exactly 90°, the bevel equivalent of a rack) and internal (over 90°).
  • Miter gears: an identical pair at 90°, each with a 45° pitch angle, so a 1:1 ratio. Zerol: spiral teeth with zero spiral angle. Skew bevel: straight teeth on non-intersecting shafts. Face gears: a spur or helical pinion on a disc gear.
  • Hypoid gears: spiral-type teeth on offset, non-intersecting shafts. The pitch surfaces are hyperboloids, so there is sliding, which needs EP oil and gives 96–98% efficiency against 98–99% for bevels. Used in automobile differentials: the offset drops the drive shaft and lowers the car's centre of gravity.

2. Terminology and Tredgold's approximation (§19.2)

  • Pitch cone, with its apex the cone centre O. Cone distance A₀ is the length of a pitch-cone element. Pitch angle γ (pinion) or Γ (gear) is measured between the pitch line and the gear's own axis. Face angle = γ + addendum angle. Root angle = γ − dedendum angle. The back cone has elements perpendicular to the pitch cone. The tooth shrinks towards O, so m, D, ha and hf are quoted at the large end.
  • The true involute profile lies on a sphere. Tredgold's approximation replaces the sphere by the back cone, a tangent cone that can be unrolled flat. That turns the large end into an equivalent spur gear of radius rb = D/(2 cos γ), so \(\boxed{z' = \dfrac{z}{\cos\gamma}}\) (Eqs 19.1–19.2).
Geometry of a 90° pair (Eqs 19.3–19.6) \[ \tan\gamma = \frac{z_p}{z_g},\quad \tan\Gamma = \frac{z_g}{z_p},\quad \gamma + \Gamma = 90°,\quad A_0 = \sqrt{\Big(\frac{D_p}{2}\Big)^2 + \Big(\frac{D_g}{2}\Big)^2} \]

Proof of the first: in the right triangle formed by O and the large-end pitch point, the two legs are Dp/2 and Dg/2, so tan γ = Dp/Dg = mzp/mzg.

3. Force analysis (§19.3)

Pinion components, at the middle of the face (Eqs 19.7–19.10) \[ r_m = \frac{D_p}{2} - \frac{b\sin\gamma}{2},\quad P_t = \frac{M_t}{r_m},\quad P_r = P_t\tan\alpha\cos\gamma,\quad P_a = P_t\tan\alpha\sin\gamma \]

Derivation: the separating force Ps = Pt tan α is perpendicular to the pitch line. That line makes γ with the pinion axis, so Ps splits into Ps cos γ perpendicular to the axis (radial) and Ps sin γ along it (axial). For a 90° pair, the gear's radial force = the pinion's axial force and the gear's axial force = the pinion's radial force. Both thrusts push the gears away from the cone centre, apart from each other (–717).

4. Derivation: Lewis equation for bevel gears (§19.4)

Derive \(S_b = mb\sigma_bY\Big(1 - \dfrac{b}{A_0}\Big)\) (Eq. 19.11).
  1. The tooth section shrinks linearly towards O. Take a slice of width dx at distance x from O. Its module is \(m_x = m\dfrac{x}{A_0}\) and its pitch radius is \(r_x = R\dfrac{x}{A_0}\), where R and m are the large-end values.
  2. Lewis for the slice: \(d(S_b) = m_x\,dx\,\sigma_bY = \dfrac{m\sigma_bY}{A_0}x\,dx\). Its torque contribution is \(dM_t = r_x\,d(S_b) = \dfrac{m\sigma_bYR}{A_0^2}x^2\,dx\).
  3. Integrate over the face, from A₀ − b to A₀: \(M_t = \dfrac{m\sigma_bYR}{A_0^2}\cdot\dfrac{A_0^3 - (A_0 - b)^3}{3} = m\sigma_bYRb\Big(1 - \dfrac{b}{A_0} + \dfrac{b^2}{3A_0^2}\Big)\).
  4. Define Sb as the equivalent force at the large-end radius, Sb = Mt/R. With b ≤ A₀/3 the last term is at most 1/27, so drop it: \(\boxed{S_b = mb\sigma_bY\Big(1 - \dfrac{b}{A_0}\Big)}\). The bracket is the bevel factor, and Y is read at z′ = z/cos γ.
  5. Because Sb is referred to the large end, compare it with \(P_t = 2M_t/D_p\), the large-end tangential force, not with Mt/rm. Choose b = 10m or A₀/3, whichever is smaller (Eq. 19.13).

5. Wear strength and effective load (§19.5–19.6)

Derive \(S_w = \dfrac{0.75\,bQD_pK}{\cos\gamma}\) with \(Q = \dfrac{2z_g}{z_g + z_p\tan\gamma}\).
  1. Apply Buckingham to the formative pair. The formative pinion diameter is \(2r_b = D_p/\cos\gamma\), so Sw = bQ(Dp/cos γ)K.
  2. Formative teeth: z′p = zp/cos γ and z′g = zg/cos Γ = zg/sin γ. Then \(Q = \dfrac{2z_g'}{z_g' + z_p'} = \dfrac{2z_g/\sin\gamma}{z_g/\sin\gamma + z_p/\cos\gamma} = \dfrac{2z_g}{z_g + z_p\tan\gamma}\).
  3. One of a bevel pair is usually overhung. It deflects, and only about three-quarters of the face carries load, hence the factor 0.75. K = 0.16(BHN/100)² for steel at 20° ().
  • Large-end force: Pt = 2Mt/Dp. Velocity factor: 6/(6 + v) for cut teeth and 5.6/(5.6 + √v) for generated teeth. Peff = CsPt/Cv.
  • Buckingham: \(P_d = \dfrac{21v(Ceb + P_t)}{21v + \sqrt{Ceb + P_t}}\), Peff = CsPt + Pd, with C = 11 400 for steel. Error e from Table 19.1: Class 1 (well-cut commercial) 0.050 mm up to 4 mm module. Class 2 (cut with great care) 0.025 mm. Class 3 (ground and lapped) 0.0125 mm up to 5 mm module ().
  • Spiral bevel (§19.7): spiral angle usually 35°, face-contact ratio over 1.25, at least 12 pinion teeth. Choose the hand so the thrust separates the gears. Teeth are always ground. They give gradual contact, more load capacity and higher speeds (–728).

6. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 19.4)

Ex. 19.4 (): 20-tooth pinion, 30-tooth gear, 20°, m = 4, b = 20 mm, steel 50C4 (Sut = 750), Class 3 teeth, 400 BHN, pinion at 500 rpm with 2.5 kW, starting torque 150%. Factors of safety in bending and pitting?
  1. tan γ = 20/30, so γ = 33.69° and z′ = 20/cos γ = 24.04, giving Y = 0.3371. Dp = 80, Dg = 120 and A₀ = √(40² + 60²) = 72.11 mm.
  2. Sb = 4(20)(250)(0.3371)(1 − 20/72.11) = 4872 N.
  3. Q = 60/(30 + 20 tan 33.69°) = 1.385 and K = 0.16(4)² = 2.56. Sw = 0.75(20)(1.385)(80)(2.56)/cos 33.69° = 5113 N.
  4. Mt = 47 746.5 N·mm, so Pt = 2Mt/Dp = 1193.7 N. v = 2.094 m/s and e = 0.0125 mm. Pd = 21(2.094)(2850 + 1193.7)/[21(2.094) + √4043.7] = 1653.3 N. Peff = 1.5(1193.7) + 1653.3 = 3443.7 N.
  5. (fs) in bending = 4872/3443.7 = 1.41. (fs) in pitting = 5113/3443.7 = 1.48.

8. GATE corner

A pair of bevel gears on shafts at 90° has 20 and 30 teeth. The pitch angle of the pinion is:

  1. 33.69°
  2. 41.81°
  3. 48.19°
  4. 56.31°
tan γ = 20/30. The gear's pitch angle is 56.31°, and the other two come from sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹ of 2/3.

Tredgold's approximation for bevel gears replaces the spherical tooth profile by a spur gear developed on the:

  1. pitch cone
  2. root cone
  3. back cone
  4. face cone
The back cone is tangent to the sphere at the large end and can be unrolled flat. Its radius rb = D/2cos γ gives z′ = z/cos γ.

In a pair of bevel gears with shafts at right angles, the axial force on the pinion equals the:

  1. tangential force on the gear tooth
  2. radial force acting on the gear
  3. axial force acting on the gear
  4. separating force divided by cos γ
The pinion axis is perpendicular to the gear axis, so the pinion's axial direction is the gear's radial direction (Fig. 19.10).

Hypoid gears are mainly used in automobile differentials because they:

  1. run with straight teeth on parallel shafts
  2. need no special lubricant despite sliding
  3. give the highest efficiency of any gear pair
  4. let the drive shaft sit below the axle centre
The offset axes lower the propeller shaft and the car's centre of gravity. Hypoids need EP oil and are slightly less efficient (§19.1).

Exam traps

  • Bearing-load questions use Pt = Mt/rm. Strength checks use Pt = 2Mt/Dp at the large end. Do not mix them.
  • rm = Dp/2 − (b sin γ)/2, using the pinion's γ for the pinion. For the gear use Γ.
  • Y at z′ = z/cos γ (not cos³), and remember the bevel factor (1 − b/A₀).
  • Wear: 0.75bQDpK/cos γ, with Q = 2zg/(zg + zp tan γ).
  • Face width: the smaller of 10m and A₀/3.
