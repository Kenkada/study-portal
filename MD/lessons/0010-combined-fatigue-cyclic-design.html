Fatigue under combined stresses: designing simple elements for cyclic loading

About 30 minutes · Bhandari §5.15 · course slides pp. 248–270

Your win: you can design a shaft or cantilever whose bending, torsion or axial load all fluctuate. You'll know both methods your course uses: Bhandari's (distortion energy, then Goodman) and the Soderberg equivalent-stress method on your professor's slides.
Warm-up (Lessons 2, 9)
Distortion energy equivalent stress for σ plus τ?
\(\sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2}\)
Goodman intersection Sa for a load line of slope tan θ?
\(S_a = 1\big/\left(\frac{1}{S_e} + \frac{1}{S_{ut}\tan\theta}\right)\)
For pure fluctuating torsion, what limits τa?
Sse/(fs). Mean stress doesn't matter until yielding.

1. Bhandari's method: combine means with means, amplitudes with amplitudes

Apply the distortion energy theory separately to the mean and the alternating components. Then treat the two results like a single fluctuating normal stress on the modified Goodman diagram ():

Two-dimensional normal stresses (Eqs 5.48, 5.49) \[ \sigma_m = \sqrt{\sigma_{xm}^2 - \sigma_{xm}\sigma_{ym} + \sigma_{ym}^2} \qquad \sigma_a = \sqrt{\sigma_{xa}^2 - \sigma_{xa}\sigma_{ya} + \sigma_{ya}^2} \]
Bending plus torsion (Eqs 5.50, 5.51) \[ \sigma_m = \sqrt{\sigma_{xm}^2 + 3\tau_{xym}^2} \qquad \sigma_a = \sqrt{\sigma_{xa}^2 + 3\tau_{xya}^2} \]

For a solid shaft: \(\sigma_{xm} = \frac{32(M_b)_m}{\pi d^3}\), \(\tau_{xym} = \frac{16(M_t)_m}{\pi d^3}\), and likewise for the amplitudes. Since everything shares \(1/d^3\), tan θ = σa/σm is known before d.

2. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 5.20, question bank Q22)

Example 5.20 (). A shaft carries a pulley midway between bearings. The bending moment at the pulley varies 200 → 600 N·m while the torque varies 70 → 200 N·m, both at shaft frequency. FeE 400 (Sut = 540, Syt = 400 N/mm²), corrected Se = 200 N/mm², (fs) = 2. Find d.
  1. \((M_b)_m = 400\), \((M_b)_a = 200\), \((M_t)_m = 135\), \((M_t)_a = 65\) N·m.
  2. \(\sigma_{xm} = \frac{4074.4\times10^3}{d^3}\), \(\sigma_{xa} = \frac{2037.2\times10^3}{d^3}\), \(\tau_m = \frac{687.6\times10^3}{d^3}\), \(\tau_a = \frac{331.0\times10^3}{d^3}\).
  3. \(\sigma_m = \sqrt{4074.4^2 + 3(687.6)^2}\times\frac{10^3}{d^3} = \frac{4244.8\times10^3}{d^3}\); \(\sigma_a = \frac{2116.3\times10^3}{d^3}\). So tan θ = 0.4986.
  4. Goodman: \(\frac{S_a}{200} + \frac{S_a/0.4986}{540} = 1 \Rightarrow S_a = 114.76\), \(S_m = 230.16\). Their sum is 345 < 400, so no yielding.
  5. \(\frac{2116.3\times10^3}{d^3} = \frac{114.76}{2}\Rightarrow d = 33.29\) mm.

3. The slides' method: Soderberg equivalent stresses

Two problems on your professor's slides (pp. 260–266) follow R S Khurmi. Each fluctuating stress becomes an equivalent static stress via the Soderberg line, with the stress concentration and correction factors loaded onto the alternating part:

Soderberg equivalent stresses \[ \sigma_{eq} = \sigma_m + \sigma_a\,\frac{S_{yt}}{S_e}\cdot\frac{K_f}{K_{sur}K_{sz}} \qquad \tau_{eq} = \tau_m + \tau_a\,\frac{S_{sy}}{S_{se}}\cdot\frac{K_{fs}}{K_{sur}K_{sz}} \]

Then combine with a static failure theory. With maximum shear stress theory: \(\tau_{max} = \frac12\sqrt{\sigma_{eq}^2 + 4\tau_{eq}^2} = \frac{S_{sy}}{(fs)}\). For a shaft this is \(\frac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{M_{eq}^2+T_{eq}^2}\).

Which method in the exam? If the question gives a corrected Se and asks for "modified Goodman", use Bhandari's. If it gives "size factor, surface factor, yield stress" in a list and no Sut-based diagram, it is the Soderberg method. When unsure, state the method in your first line.

Slides p. 266. A hot-rolled steel shaft: torque varies from 330 N·m clockwise to 110 N·m anticlockwise; bending moment at the critical section varies from 440 to −220 N·m. Sut = 550, Syt = 410 MPa, Se = 0.5Sut, (fs) = 2, size factor 0.85, surface factor 0.62, no keyway. Find d.
  1. Tm = ½(330 − 110) = 110 and Ta = ½(330 + 110) = 220 N·m. Mm = 110 and Ma = 330 N·m.
  2. Se = 275, Ssy = 0.5(410) = 205, Sse = 0.5(275) = 137.5 MPa, KsurKsz = 0.527.
  3. \(T_{eq} = 110 + 220\cdot\frac{205}{137.5}\cdot\frac{1}{0.527} = 732.4\) N·m; \(M_{eq} = 110 + 330\cdot\frac{410}{275}\cdot\frac{1}{0.527} = 1043.6\) N·m.
  4. \(\frac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{1043.6^2 + 732.4^2}\times10^3 = \frac{205}{2}\Rightarrow d = 39.9\) mm, so use 40 mm.

4. A procedure for any simple element under cyclic load

  1. Critical section: largest moment, smallest section, or a stress raiser. Check each candidate.
  2. Load variation: max and min of every load, then the mean and amplitude of each (watch the signs).
  3. Se at that section: \(K_aK_bK_cK_d(0.5S_{ut})\); 0.8 for axial; 0.5 or 0.577 for torsion.
  4. Criterion: reversed → Se/(fs) or the S–N line; fluctuating normal → modified Goodman; fluctuating shear → Sse; combined → distortion energy on means and amplitudes, then Goodman (or Soderberg equivalents).
  5. Size, then check yielding and any assumed Kb range.

5. Practice

Bhandari Ex. 5.19: σx varies 40 → 100 N/mm² and σy varies 10 → 80 N/mm², in phase. Corrected Se = 270, Sut = 660 N/mm². Find the factor of safety (Goodman).

σxm = 70, σxa = 30, σym = 45, σya = 35.

\(\sigma_m = \sqrt{70^2 - 70(45) + 45^2} = 61.44\); \(\sigma_a = \sqrt{30^2 - 30(35) + 35^2} = 32.79\); tan θ = 0.534.

\(S_a = 1/(1/270 + 1/(660\times0.534)) = 152.9\). (fs) = 152.9/32.79 = 4.66.

Slides p. 260 (Soderberg method): a 200 mm steel cantilever is 2d in diameter for the first 50 mm from the support and d for the rest. Axial load varies 150 N compression → 450 N tension. Transverse end load varies 80 N up → 120 N down. Syt = 330 MPa; Se = 300 MPa (bending) with factor 0.7 for axial; Kt = 1.44 (bending), 1.64 (axial); q = 0.9; size 0.85; surface 0.90; (fs) = 2. Find d.

Critical section: the shoulder, 150 mm from the free end. Pm = 150, Pa = 300 N. Wm = 20, Wa = 100 N, so Mm = 3000, Ma = 15 000 N·mm.

Kf (bending) = 1.396, Kf (axial) = 1.576, KsurKsz = 0.765.

Axial: \(\sigma_{eq} = \frac{191}{d^2} + \frac{382}{d^2}\cdot\frac{330}{210}\cdot\frac{1.576}{0.765} = \frac{1428}{d^2}\). Bending: \(\sigma_{eq} = \frac{30\,558}{d^3} + \frac{152\,789}{d^3}\cdot\frac{330}{300}\cdot\frac{1.396}{0.765} = \frac{337\,250}{d^3}\).

\(\frac{1428}{d^2} + \frac{337\,250}{d^3} = \frac{330}{2}\Rightarrow d \approx 12.9\) mm (trial and error), so use 13 mm.

In Bhandari's combined-stress method, how are mean and alternating components treated?

  1. Both are added first, then the theory is applied
  2. Only alternating parts are combined by the theory
  3. Each set is combined separately by distortion energy
  4. Each is checked separately, with no combination
Eqs 5.50–5.51 apply \(\sqrt{\sigma^2+3\tau^2}\) once to the means and once to the amplitudes. The two resulting stresses then go onto the modified Goodman diagram.

In the Soderberg equivalent-stress method, which part is multiplied by Kf/(KsurKsz)?

  1. The alternating component only
  2. The mean component only
  3. Both mean and alternating parts
  4. Neither; they reduce Syt
Stress concentration, size and surface affect fatigue (the alternating part), not static strength. So they scale σa only: \(\sigma_{eq} = \sigma_m + \sigma_a(S_{yt}/S_e)K_f/(K_{sur}K_{sz})\).

Exam traps

  • Combining a mean stress with an amplitude in the same square root.
  • Sign errors in "330 clockwise to 110 anticlockwise". One of them is negative.
  • Forgetting that the load line's tan θ comes from the combined σa/σm, not from the bending moments alone.
