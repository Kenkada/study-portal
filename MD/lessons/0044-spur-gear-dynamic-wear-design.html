Unit 4 · Lesson 44

Spur gears: dynamic load, wear strength and design

About 45 minutes · Bhandari §17.19–17.24

Your win: you can find the effective load with the service factor and either Barth's velocity factor or Buckingham's dynamic-load equation. You can derive the module from beam strength and the wear strength from Hertz contact stress, then design a spur pair and specify its surface hardness. That is the standard 15-mark gear problem.
Warm-up (Lesson 43)
Lewis beam strength and the permissible stress Bhandari uses?
Sb = mbσbY with σb = Sut/3.
How do you pick the weaker of pinion and gear?
The smaller σb·Y. With the same material it is the pinion.
Which failure does "wear strength" guard against?
Destructive pitting, a surface fatigue failure from contact stress.

1. Effective load: service factor and dynamic load (§17.19)

Service factor Cs = maximum torque / rated torque. It covers the torque peaks of the driver and the driven machine (Table 17.4). For an electric motor, Cs = starting torque / rated torque, so "starting torque 150%" means Cs = 1.5 (–675).

Dynamic load comes from profile and spacing errors, misalignment, elastic deflection and inertia. It can be estimated two ways (–677):

Preliminary design: Barth's velocity factor (Eqs 17.20–17.24) \[ C_v = \frac{3}{3 + v}\ (\text{cut gears},\ v < 10) \qquad \frac{6}{6 + v}\ (\text{hobbed, generated},\ v < 20) \qquad \frac{5.6}{5.6 + \sqrt v}\ (\text{precision},\ v > 20) \] \[ v = \frac{\pi d'n}{60\times10^3}\ \text{m/s},\qquad P_{eff} = \frac{C_sP_t}{C_v} \]
Final check: Buckingham's dynamic load (Eqs 17.25–17.29) \[ P_d = \frac{21v\,(Ceb + P_t)}{21v + \sqrt{Ceb + P_t}},\qquad P_{eff} = C_sP_t + P_d \]

C is the deformation factor, k/(1/Ep + 1/Eg): 11 400 N/mm² for steel on steel (20° full depth), 7900 for steel on CI, 5700 for CI on CI. The error e = ep + eg. For each gear e (μm) = A + Bφ, with φ = m + 0.25√d′. Grade 4: 3.2 + 0.25φ. Grade 6: 8 + 0.63φ. Grade 8: 16 + 1.25φ. Convert μm to mm before use.

The velocity factor is simple and has a long service record (AGMA), but it ignores masses, elasticity and accuracy. Buckingham's Pd is often 3–4 times Pt and overestimates for small gears, small shafts (under 50 mm) and small powers (under 15 kW).

2. Derivation: estimating the module from beam strength (Eq. 17.30)

Derive \(m = \Big[\dfrac{60\times10^6}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{(\text{kW})\,C_s\,(fs)}{z\,n\,C_v\,(b/m)\,\sigma_b\,Y}\Big]^{1/3}\).
  1. \(P_t = \dfrac{2M_t}{d'} = \dfrac{2}{mz}\cdot\dfrac{60\times10^6(\text{kW})}{2\pi n} = \dfrac{60\times10^6(\text{kW})}{\pi mzn}\), so \(P_{eff} = \dfrac{C_sP_t}{C_v}\).
  2. With b = (b/m)·m: \(S_b = mb\sigma_bY = m^2\,(b/m)\,\sigma_bY\).
  3. Set Sb = Peff(fs): \(m^2(b/m)\sigma_bY = \dfrac{60\times10^6(\text{kW})C_s(fs)}{\pi mznC_v}\), which gives the result. Use the z, n and σbY of the weaker gear. Cv needs v, which needs m, so assume v ≈ 5 m/s first, then check.

3. Derivation: Buckingham's wear strength from Hertz (§17.21)

Derive Sw = bQd′pK, with \(Q = \dfrac{2z_g}{z_g + z_p}\) and \(K = \dfrac{\sigma_c^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{1.4}\Big(\dfrac1{E_1} + \dfrac1{E_2}\Big)\).
  1. Two cylinders of radii r₁ and r₂ pressed together by P over length l (Hertz, ν = 0.3): \(\sigma_c^2 = \dfrac{0.35\,(P/l)\,(1/r_1 + 1/r_2)}{1/E_1 + 1/E_2}\).
  2. Pitting starts near the pitch line, where one pair carries the load. So use the involute radii of curvature at the pitch point: \(r = \tfrac12 d'\sin\alpha\), giving \(\dfrac1{r_1} + \dfrac1{r_2} = \dfrac{2}{\sin\alpha}\Big(\dfrac1{d_p'} + \dfrac1{d_g'}\Big) = \dfrac{4}{Q\,d_p'\sin\alpha}\), with \(Q = \dfrac{2d_g'}{d_g' + d_p'} = \dfrac{2z_g}{z_g + z_p}\).
  3. The normal force is P = Pt/cos α and the length is l = b: \(\sigma_c^2 = \dfrac{1.4P_t}{b\,Q\,d_p'\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,(1/E_1 + 1/E_2)}\).
  4. Rearrange: Pt = bQd′pK. When σc equals the surface endurance strength, Pt is the largest force without pitting: \(\boxed{S_w = bQd_p'K}\).
  5. Both gears steel (E = 206 000), α = 20°, and Niemann's σc = 0.27(9.81)(BHN): \(\boxed{K = 0.16\Big(\dfrac{\text{BHN}}{100}\Big)^2}\) N/mm². For internal gears, Q = 2zg/(zg − zp), which is larger, so internal gears are stronger in wear (–680, 689).

Why harder gears? Sw ∝ BHN², but Sb does not depend on hardness at all. Hardening a gear fixes pitting, not tooth breakage. Bigger modules fix breakage.

4. The design procedure

  1. zp = 18 to 20 (20° full depth), zg = izp. Find the weaker gear (σbY).
  2. Assume v = 5 m/s, giving Cv. Take b = 10m and estimate m from Eq. 17.30. Round up to a standard module.
  3. Recompute d′, v, Cv, Pt and Peff. Check (fs) = Sb/Peff ≥ 1.5 to 2. If it fails, go to the next module.
  4. Final check with Buckingham's Pd for the chosen manufacturing grade. If the design fails, choose a finer grade or a larger module (Ex. 17.9).
  5. Surface hardness from Sw = Peff(fs): BHN = 100√(K/0.16).
  6. List the dimensions: m, b, z, d′, da = m(z + 2), df = m(z − 2.5), clearance 0.25m, fillet 0.4m.

Internal gears (§17.23): a compact, smooth drive that uses more of the tooth face. The internal tooth is the shape of an external tooth space, so it is thicker. It is stronger in both bending and wear, so design only the pinion. Keep zg − zp ≥ 10 for 20° full depth (8 for stub, 12 for 14.5°). Lubrication (§17.24): grease for hand-operated, slow gears. A mineral oil bath (SAE 80/90/140, splash) at medium speed, a spray at high speed, and EP oils for heavy-duty boxes (–690).

5. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 17.7)

Ex. 17.7 (): 10 kW, 1440 rpm motor with starting torque 150%, reduction 4:1, both gears 40C8 (Sut = 600), (fs) = 1.5, 20° full depth, Lewis equation with velocity factor. Design the gears and suggest a surface hardness.
  1. Same material, so the pinion is weaker. zp = 18 and zg = 72. Y = 0.308, Cs = 1.5, Mt = 66 314.6 N·mm.
  2. Assume v = 5 m/s: Cv = 3/8. With b/m = 10 and σb = 200: \(m = \Big[\dfrac{60\times10^6}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{10(1.5)(1.5)}{18(1440)(3/8)(10)(200)(0.308)}\Big]^{1/3} = 4.16\) mm, so take m = 5 mm.
  3. d′p = 90, d′g = 360 and b = 50 mm. Pt = 2(66 314.6)/90 = 1473.7 N. v = π(90)(1440)/60 000 = 6.786 m/s, so Cv = 3/9.786 = 0.3066.
  4. Peff = 1.5(1473.7)/0.3066 = 7210 N. Sb = 5(50)(200)(0.308) = 15 400 N. (fs) = 15 400/7210 = 2.14 ≥ 1.5 ✓.
  5. Q = 2(72)/90 = 1.6. Sw = 50(1.6)(90)(0.16)(BHN/100)² = 1152(BHN/100)². Set this equal to 7210 × 1.5: BHN = 306.4, so specify 310 BHN.

6. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 17.8 (): 22.5 kW, 1450 rpm motor (starting torque 150%), pinion 20 teeth of Fe 410, gear 50 teeth of FG 200 (the gear is weaker), (fs) = 1.5. Bhandari gets m = 6.89 and picks m = 7 (Choice 2) with hobbed teeth (Cv = 6/(6 + v)). Actual factor of safety?

ng = 580 rpm and Mt (gear) = 370 446.9 N·mm. d′g = 350, so Pt = 2116.8 N. v = π(140)(1450)/60 000 = 10.63 m/s, so Cv = 6/16.63 = 0.3608. Peff = 1.5(2116.8)/0.3608 = 8800 N. Sb = 7(70)(200/3)(0.408) = 13 328 N. (fs) = 1.51.

Ex. 17.9 (): 19-tooth pinion on a 7.5 kW, 1500 rpm single-cylinder diesel, 40-tooth gear, Cs = 1.5, steel Sut = 600, m = 4, b = 40 mm, cut teeth. Factor of safety with the velocity factor?

Sb = 4(40)(200)(0.314) = 10 048 N. d′p = 76, Pt = 1256.5 N, v = 5.969 m/s, Cv = 0.3345. Peff = 5634.5 N, so (fs) = 1.78. For (fs) = 2 in pitting: Q = 1.356, so BHN = 413 → 420.

Ex. 17.9 continued: the gears are made to Grade 8. Buckingham's dynamic load?

Pinion: φ = 4 + 0.25√76 = 6.18, so ep = 16 + 1.25(6.18) = 23.72 μm. Gear (d′ = 160): eg = 24.95 μm. e = 48.67 μm = 0.04867 mm. Ceb = 11 400(0.04867)(40) = 22 193. Pd = 21(5.969)(22 193 + 1256.5)/[21(5.969) + √23 449.5] = 10 555 N. Peff = 1.5(1256.5) + 10 555 = 12 440 N, which exceeds both Sb and Sw ✗. The cure: Grade 6 (e = 24.4 μm) gives Pd = 6560.5 and (fs) = 10 048/8445 = 1.19 ✓.

Ex. 17.10 (): 20/41 teeth, m = 3, b = 40, steel Sut = 600 hardened to 400 BHN, pinion 1450 rpm, Cs = 1.75, (fs) = 1.5, velocity factor 3/(3 + v). Rated power?

Sb = 7680 N. Q = 82/61 = 1.344 and K = 0.16(4)² = 2.56, so Sw = 40(1.344)(60)(2.56) = 8258 N. Beam strength is lower, so it governs. v = 4.555 m/s and Cv = 0.397, so Peff = 1.75Pt/0.397 = 4.41Pt. Then 7680 = 1.5(4.41Pt) gives Pt = 1161 N. Mt = 1161 × 30 = 34 830 N·mm, so kW = 5.29.

Ex. 17.11 (): 7.5 kW, 1000 → 250 rpm, a = 250 mm, steel Sut = 700, Cs = 1.5, preliminary (fs) = 2. The design gives m = 4 (z = 25/100, b = 40) and Grade 6 teeth. Actual factor of safety against bending, using Buckingham?

Sb = 4(40)(700/3)(0.34) = 12 693 N. Pt = 1432.4 N and v = 5.236 m/s. ep = 8 + 0.63(4 + 0.25√100) = 12.095 μm and eg = 13.67 μm, so e = 0.025765 mm. Pd = 6448.3 N and Peff = 1.5(1432.4) + 6448.3 = 8596.9 N. (fs) = 1.48. Hardness for the same (fs) in pitting: Q = 1.6, so BHN = 352.5 → 360.

7. GATE corner

Buckingham's wear strength equation for gears is based on:

  1. Lewis bending stress at the tooth root
  2. fatigue of the gear material in shear
  3. Hertz contact stress at the pitch point
  4. frictional heating between the meshing teeth
Two cylinders with the involute radii at the pitch point, pressed by Pt/cos α (§17.21).

Raising the surface hardness of a steel gear pair from 300 to 400 BHN increases:

  1. the wear strength by about 78%
  2. the beam strength by about 33%
  3. both strengths by about 33%
  4. neither strength, only the wear life
K ∝ BHN², so (400/300)² = 1.78. The Lewis beam strength depends on Sut/3, not on surface hardness.

For the same tooth numbers, the ratio factor Q of an internal gear pair compared with an external pair is:

  1. smaller, so internal gears wear sooner
  2. equal, since Q depends only on module
  3. smaller, but the face width is larger
  4. larger, so the wear strength is higher
Q = 2i/(i − 1) for internal and 2i/(i + 1) for external pairs. A concave flank meeting a convex one also gives more contact area.

The velocity factor Cv = 3/(3 + v) applies to:

  1. precision ground gears above 20 m/s
  2. ordinary cut gears below 10 m/s
  3. hobbed generated gears below 20 m/s
  4. any gears, whatever the pitch line velocity
3/(3 + v) for form-cut gears up to 10 m/s, 6/(6 + v) for hobbed or generated gears up to 20 m/s, and 5.6/(5.6 + √v) for precision gears.

Exam traps

  • Pitch-line velocity is the same for both gears. Compute it from the pinion's d′ and n.
  • Buckingham uses the rated Pt inside the formula. Cs appears only in Peff = CsPt + Pd.
  • Error e is in microns in Table 17.8. Multiply by 10⁻³ before using it.
  • K = 0.16(BHN/100)² is valid only for steel on steel at 20°. For other materials use Eq. 17.32 with σc and the two E values.
  • In the wear check, use the pinion's d′ (d′p) in Sw = bQd′pK, even when the gear was the weaker one in bending.
