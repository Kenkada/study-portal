1. Types of loading

A suddenly applied load (h = 0) causes twice the stress of the same load applied gradually ().

2. Design criteria: what "failure" means for this part

Bhandari names three modes of failure: elastic deflection, general yielding, and fracture (). Each design criterion guards against one of them, or against a practical failure such as an unmaintainable part.

CriterionRequirementProperty that mattersTypical parts
StrengthStress ≤ allowable: \(\sigma \le S_{yt}/(fs)\) (ductile) or \(S_{ut}/(fs)\) (brittle) or \(S_e/(fs)\) (fatigue)Syt, Sut, SeJoints, levers, axles, brackets
Stiffness (rigidity)Deflection or twist ≤ permissible value. Stress is low and often irrelevant.E and G, not strength ()Machine-tool spindles, gear shafts (alignment), long shafts (twist ≤ 0.25°/m)
ResilienceAbsorb energy elastically and give it back without permanent set. Modulus of resilience \(u = S_{yt}^2/2E\).High Syt, lower ESprings, bolts under shock, buffers
Production and maintenanceEasy to make, assemble, inspect, service and replace at low life-cycle cost.Castability, machinability, weldability; standard partsEvery product. Covered by DFMA (Bhandari §3.7) and use of standards

Resilience vs toughness (): resilience is energy absorbed in the elastic range (area under the stress–strain curve up to yield). Toughness is the total energy absorbed up to fracture (the whole area). Springs need resilience. Parts under impact need toughness.

3. Factor of safety

Definition (§4.2) \[ (fs) = \frac{\text{failure stress}}{\text{allowable stress}} = \frac{\text{failure load}}{\text{working load}} \]

It covers uncertainty in loads, scatter in material properties, imperfect workmanship and simplifying assumptions. Bhandari's guide values (–79):

SituationBased on(fs)
Cast iron (brittle, non-homogeneous, residual stresses)Sut3 – 5
Ductile material, static loadSyt1.5 – 2
Ductile material, fluctuating loadcorrected Se1.3 – 1.5
Contact stress: gears, cams, rolling bearings (pitting)surface endurance limit1.8 – 2.5
Buckling: piston rods, power screws, studscritical buckling load3 – 6
Simple parts designed only on direct stresses (Bhandari §4.8)Sytup to 5

Use a higher (fs) when: loads are uncertain or impact is present, the material is non-homogeneous, residual stresses or stress concentration exist, the environment is corrosive or hot, failure endangers life (hoists, boilers), the part can't be tested in service, high reliability is demanded, or manufacturing quality is poor (). The cost: a higher (fs) means bigger, heavier, costlier parts.

4. Worked example: strength says 44 mm, stiffness says 74 mm

Criterion check. A 1 m long steel shaft carries a torque of 1000 N·m. Allowable shear stress 60 N/mm²; permissible twist 0.25° per metre; G = 80 000 N/mm².
  1. Strength. \(60 = \dfrac{16(1000\times10^3)}{\pi d^3}\Rightarrow d = 43.9\) mm.
  2. Stiffness. \(0.25 = \dfrac{584\,(1000\times10^3)(1000)}{80\,000\, d^4}\Rightarrow d = 73.5\) mm.
  3. Decision. Stiffness governs, so use d = 75 mm. At that size the shear stress is only about 12 N/mm². This is Bhandari's point: when rigidity governs, "the stresses induced are not significant" ().

5. Practice

A 1000 N weight falls 10 mm onto a collar at the end of a steel bar 1000 mm long with area 500 mm². E = 207 000 N/mm². Find the impact stress.

\(\frac{2hAE}{Wl} = \frac{2(10)(500)(207\,000)}{1000(1000)} = 2070\).

\(\sigma_i = \frac{1000}{500}\left[1+\sqrt{2071}\right] = 2(1+45.5) = 93.0\) N/mm². The shock factor is 46.5: a 1 kN weight dropped 10 mm acts like a 46.5 kN static load.

Find the modulus of resilience of a steel with \(S_{yt}=380\) N/mm² and E = 207 000 N/mm².

\(u = S_{yt}^2/2E = 380^2/(2\times207\,000) = 0.349\) N·mm/mm³ (= MJ/m³).

A 10 kN load is suddenly applied (no drop height) to a bar of 100 mm² cross-section. What is the maximum stress?

With h = 0: \(\sigma_i = 2W/A = 2(10\,000)/100 = 200\) N/mm², twice the gradually applied value.

A lathe spindle must hold the work accurately under cutting forces. Which criterion usually governs its size?

  1. Strength, using the yield strength of the steel
  2. Resilience, using the modulus of resilience
  3. Stiffness, using the permissible elastic deflection
  4. Production, using the cost of machining it
Accuracy is lost through deflection, long before the spindle is anywhere near yielding. The size comes from load-deflection equations, so E and G matter, not \(S_{yt}\) ().

Which situation calls for the largest factor of safety on Bhandari's list?

  1. A steel bracket under steady static load
  2. A steel shaft under fully reversed bending
  3. A hardened gear checked for surface pitting
  4. A slender piston rod checked for buckling
Buckling: 3 to 6 on the critical load. Static ductile: 1.5–2. Fatigue on the corrected endurance limit: 1.3–1.5. Contact stress: 1.8–2.5.

How does toughness differ from resilience?

  1. Toughness includes energy absorbed in plastic range
  2. Toughness counts only energy stored elastically
  3. Toughness is measured by the elastic modulus
  4. Toughness is required mainly for spring materials
Modulus of toughness is the whole area under the stress–strain curve, up to fracture. Modulus of resilience is the area up to yield only. Springs need resilience. Impact-loaded parts need toughness.

Exam traps

  • Designing only for strength when the question also gives a deflection or twist limit. Size for both and pick the larger.
  • Forgetting the factor of 2 for a suddenly applied load.
  • Listing factors that affect (fs) without saying which way each one pushes it.
