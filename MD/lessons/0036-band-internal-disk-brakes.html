1. Internal expanding shoe brake: derivation

A shoe pivoted at one end is pushed outward against the inside of the drum at the other end (by a hydraulic cylinder or cam). It is used on automobiles (two shoes), conveyors and hoists. For: simple, cheap, reliable, little maintenance, small effort for large torque, sealed from dirt. Against: poor heat dissipation; may self-lock as it wears if badly designed ().

Derive Eqs 12.16–12.23: angles φ are measured from the line joining the drum centre to the pivot (distance h); the lining runs from θ₁ to θ₂; C is the arm of P about the pivot.
  1. The shoe rotates about its pivot, so the deformation (and pressure) at a point is ∝ its distance from the pivot measured perpendicular to the radius, h sin φ. So \(p = p_{max}\dfrac{\sin\varphi}{\sin\varphi_{max}}\), with φmax = 90° if θ₂ ≥ 90°, otherwise θ₂.
  2. Element: dN = pRw dφ. Moment arms about the pivot: normal force h sin φ; friction force (R − h cos φ).
  3. \(M_n = \int dN\,h\sin\varphi = \dfrac{p_{max}Rwh}{4\sin\varphi_{max}}\left[2(\theta_2-\theta_1) - (\sin2\theta_2 - \sin2\theta_1)\right]\).
  4. \(M_f = \int\mu\,dN(R - h\cos\varphi) = \dfrac{\mu p_{max}Rw}{4\sin\varphi_{max}}\left[4R(\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_2) - h(\cos2\theta_1-\cos2\theta_2)\right]\).
  5. Torque: \(M_t = \dfrac{\mu R^2 p_{max}w(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)}{\sin\varphi_{max}}\).
  6. Moments on the shoe about the pivot: leading shoe (friction moment adds to P's moment): \(P = \dfrac{M_n - M_f}{C}\), self-energising. Trailing shoe: \(P = \dfrac{M_n + M_f}{C}\), which needs more effort. Self-locking if Mf ≥ Mn.
  7. With the same P on both shoes, the trailing shoe develops less pressure (p′max = P·C/(Mn + Mf) per unit pmax), so it carries less torque. Braking in reverse is weaker. Ergonomics: pedal force 22–25 N (11–17 N with a booster), pedal travel ≤ 150 mm, design for 1g deceleration ().

2. Band brakes

Simple and differential band brake (Eqs 12.24–12.28) \[ \frac{P_1}{P_2} = e^{\mu\theta} \qquad M_t = (P_1 - P_2)R \qquad p_{max} = \frac{P_1}{Rw} \]

Simple band (slack end at the fulcrum, arm a): P = P₂a/l. Differential band (ends at arms b and a on either side of the fulcrum): \(P = \dfrac{P_2a - P_1b}{l} = \dfrac{P_2(a - b\,e^{\mu\theta})}{l}\), self-locking if a ≤ b·eμθ.

The pressure under the band varies with the local tension (dN = P dφ, so p = P/Rw), and is highest at the tight end. Self-locking is avoided in speed-control brakes but used deliberately in back-stop brakes: the drum turns freely one way and locks if it tries to reverse (bucket elevators, hoists after a power failure). For: simple, few parts, easily made, reliable. Against: poor heat dissipation, uneven lining wear. Applications: bucket conveyors, hoists, chain saws, back-stops (–492).

3. Caliper disc brake

Annular pad, uniform pressure (Eqs 12.29–12.33) \[ R_f = \frac{2}{3}\frac{R_o^3 - R_i^3}{R_o^2 - R_i^2} \qquad M_t = \mu P R_f\ \text{per pad face} \qquad A = \tfrac12\theta(R_o^2 - R_i^2) \qquad P = p_{av}A \]

Circular pad of radius R at distance e: Rf = δe (δ = 0.9693 for R/e = 0.2, Table 12.1).

Advantages of disc brakes (): simple to install and service, high torque in a small volume, good heat dissipation (the pad covers only part of the disc), no fade, can never self-lock, equally effective in both directions, braking torque linear in actuating force, balanced axial force (no bearing thrust). Applications: motorcycle front wheels, cars, lift trucks, farm machinery.

Thermal check (§12.9) \[ \Delta t = \frac{E}{mc} \qquad \text{or limit } pv\ \text{(N/mm}^2\cdot\text{m/min): 115 intermittent, 175 continuous with good cooling, 125 vehicles} \]

4. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 12.8 (): automotive double-shoe brake, w = 40, pmax = 1, μ = 0.32, R = 125, h = 100, θ₁ = 0, θ₂ = 120°, C = 187.5 mm. Actuating force (from the leading shoe)?

sin φmax = 1. Mf = 0.32(125)(40)[4(125)(1.5) − 100(1.5)]/4 = 240 000; Mn = (125)(40)(100)[2(2.094) + 0.866]/4 = 631 852 N·mm.

P = (631 852 − 240 000)/187.5 = 2089.9 N. Torque: leading shoe 300 000; trailing p′max = 0.45, so 135 000; total 435 N·m.

Ex. 12.10 (): differential band brake, band 100 × 3 mm, σ ≤ 50, μ = 0.25, wrap 240°, R = 300; band ends at 50 mm (tight) and 200 mm (slack) from the fulcrum; lever 950 mm. Actuating force?

P₁ = 50 × 100 × 3 = 15 000 N; e0.25(4.189) = 2.85, so P₂ = 5264 N. P = (5264 × 200 − 15 000 × 50)/950 = 318.7 N. Mt = (15 000 − 5264)(0.3) = 2921 N·m. a/b = 4 > 2.85, so not self-locking.

Ex. 12.11 (): back-stop band brake with wrap 240°; geometry requires P₁/P₂ > 87.5/50 = 1.75 to lock reverse rotation. Minimum μ?

eμ(4.189) > 1.75 ⇒ μ > ln 1.75/4.189 = 0.1336. With pmax = 0.3, R = 150, w = 75: P₁ = 3375, P₂ = 1929 N, so torque = 217 N·m.

Ex. 12.12 (): caliper disc brake, 1500 N·m, 2 pads, Ro = 150, Ri = 100, μ = 0.35, pav = 2 MPa. Angular size of each pad?

Per pad 750 N·m. Rf = 126.67 mm, so P = 750 000/(0.35 × 126.67) = 16 917 N. A = P/2 = 8458 mm² = ½θ(150² − 100²) ⇒ θ = 1.353 rad = 77.5°, use 80°.

Ex. 12.14 (): a 100 kg flywheel (k = 350 mm) at 500 rpm is stopped; brake drum assembly 5 kg of cast iron, c = 460 J/kg°C, absorbing all the heat. Temperature rise?

E = ½(100)(0.35²)(52.36²) = 16 792 J, so Δt = 16 792/(5 × 460) = 7.3 °C. (Bhandari prints E = 167 921 J and 73 °C, a factor-of-10 slip.)

5. GATE corner

In a band brake, the maximum pressure between band and drum occurs:

  1. at the slack end of the band
  2. uniformly along the whole band
  3. at the middle of the arc of contact
  4. at the tight end of the band
p = P/(Rw) follows the local band tension, which is largest (P₁) at the tight end.

A differential band brake is self-locking when (a = slack-end arm, b = tight-end arm):

  1. a ≥ b eμθ
  2. a ≤ b eμθ
  3. a = b
  4. b ≤ a eμθ
P = P₂(a − b eμθ)/l ≤ 0 when a ≤ b eμθ.

Why does an automobile drum brake brake less effectively in reverse?

  1. The leading shoe becomes a trailing shoe and loses self-energising action
  2. Hydraulic pressure is lower when the car moves backwards
  3. The coefficient of friction depends on the direction of sliding
  4. The drum expands more when it rotates in reverse
In forward motion, friction on the leading shoe helps P (P = (Mn − Mf)/C). Reversing swaps the roles ().

"Brake fade" means:

  1. the lining wearing unevenly from end to end
  2. the brake locking up as the lining wears
  3. loss of friction (torque) at high temperature
  4. a gradual drop in hydraulic fluid pressure
μ falls as the lining heats up. Disc brakes cool better and resist fade.

Exam traps

  • Measuring φ from the wrong line in shoe brakes. Measure from the drum centre to the pivot.
  • Band brake: θ in radians, and P₁ is at the end where the drum drags the band away (it depends on the direction of rotation).
  • Disc brake: torque per pad times the number of pads (and of calipers).
