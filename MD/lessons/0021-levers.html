Design of levers

About 25 minutes · Bhandari §4.19–4.20

Your win: you can design a lever in three steps: forces (including the fulcrum reaction), arm cross-section from bending, and fulcrum pin from bearing pressure, checked for shear. This applies to the bell-crank lever, safety-valve lever and compound lever mechanisms (Tutorial Q7).

1. Levers: types and forces

A lever is a rigid bar pivoted at a fulcrum to multiply or transfer force: \(F\,l_2 = P\,l_1\). Mechanical advantage F/P = leverage l₁/l₂ ().

2. The three design steps (§4.20)

  1. Force analysis: P from moments about the fulcrum; R by vector sum.
  2. Lever arm: maximum bending moment at the boss (M = P × (arm up to the critical section)). Rectangular section with d = 2b to 3b (or as given): \(\sigma_b = \dfrac{M(d/2)}{bd^3/12}\). Elliptical: \(I = \pi ba^3/64\) with a = 2b. Taper the arm towards the end, since M falls to zero there.
  3. Fulcrum pin: bearing pressure decides the size, because the lever oscillates on it: \(R = p\,(d_1 l_1)\), with l₁/d₁ = 1 to 2 and p = 5–10 N/mm² for a phosphor-bronze bush. Then check double shear: \(\tau = R/(2\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}d_1^2)\). Boss OD ≈ 2d₁. Check bending at the boss through the pin hole.

Bearing pressure vs crushing stress. The formulas look the same (force/projected area), but bearing pressure applies where surfaces move relative to each other (pin in an oscillating lever, journal in a bearing, screw in a nut), so it is kept low, about 10 N/mm². Crushing applies where there is no relative motion (cotter on spigot, knuckle pin in eye), so it can be about 150 N/mm² ().

3. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 4.16)

Example 4.16 (). A right-angled bell-crank lever raises 5 kN at the end of its 100 mm short arm; the long arm is 450 mm. 30C8 (Syt = 400), (fs) = 5. Bearing pressure 10 N/mm²; section d = 3b; fulcrum pin l/d = 1.25. The bending arm runs up to the fulcrum axis.
  1. σ = 80, τ = 40 N/mm².
  2. Effort: 5000 × 100 = P × 450 ⇒ P = 1111.1 N. Reaction: R = √(5000² + 1111.1²) = 5122 N.
  3. Pin: 5122 = 10 × d₁ × 1.25d₁ ⇒ d₁ = 20.24 mm, l₁ = 25.3 mm. Shear check: 5122/(2 × π/4 × 20.24²) = 7.96 < 40 ✓.
  4. Boss: ID 21, OD 42, length 26 mm.
  5. Lever section: M = 5000 × 100 = 5 × 10⁵ N·mm. \(80 = \dfrac{5\times10^5(1.5b)}{b(3b)^3/12}\Rightarrow b = 16.09\), d = 48.3 mm.

4. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 4.15 (): lever-loaded safety valveBlow-off at 1.5 MPa; valve opening 50 mm diameter; dead weight 1000 mm from the fulcrum; valve spindle pin 100 mm from the fulcrum. 30C8, (fs) = 5, pin bearing pressure 25 N/mm², pin l = d, lever section d = 3b.

(a) Dead weight P.

F = (π/4)(50²)(1.5) = 2945.2 N. P = 2945.2 × 100/1000 = 294.5 N. Reaction R = F − P = 2650.7 N (second-type lever).

(b) Diameter of the pin at the valve spindle (it carries the largest force, F).

2945.2 = 25 × d₁² ⇒ d₁ = 10.85, so 12 mm. Shear: 2945/(2 × π/4 × 144) = 13.0 < 40 ✓.

(c) Lever width b, with maximum moment at the spindle axis: M = 294.5 × (1000 − 100).

M = 265 068 N·mm. 80 = 265 068(1.5b)/(b(3b)³/12) ⇒ b = 13.02, so 15 mm, d = 45 mm. Checking the section through the pin hole gives 49.4 < 80 ✓.

Ex. 4.18 (): bench shearing machineShears 6.25 mm mild-steel bars (Sus = 350). The block is pivoted at A with the bar 100 mm and the link 400 mm from A. The link connects to a lever pivoted at D: link at 100 mm and hand effort at 1000 mm from D. FeE 250, (fs) = 5; pins l/d = 1.25, p = 10 N/mm²; lever section h = 2b.

(a) Hand effort P.

Ps = (π/4)(6.25²)(350) = 10 738 N. Link force P₁ = 10 738 × 100/400 = 2684.5 N. Effort P = 2684.5 × 100/1000 = 268.45 N.

(b) Pin diameter (all pins identical, designed for 2684.5 N).

2684.5 = 10 × d × 1.25d ⇒ d = 14.65, so 15 mm (l = 20 mm). Shear 7.6 < 25 ✓. Link: σ = 50, so d = 8.27 → 10 mm.

(c) Lever width b (maximum moment at the link pin C: M = 268.45 × 900).

M = 241 605. 50 = 241 605(b)/((2/3)b⁴) ⇒ b = 19.35, so 20 mm, h = 40 mm.

Why is the fulcrum pin sized on bearing pressure rather than crushing stress?

  1. The pin is made of a softer material than the lever
  2. The lever oscillates on the pin, so the surfaces slide
  3. Bearing pressure always gives a smaller pin diameter
  4. Crushing stress applies only to cast-iron components
With relative motion, a low allowable bearing pressure (5–10 N/mm²) limits wear and keeps the lubricant film. Crushing stress, much higher, is for surfaces that don't move.

A lever-loaded boiler safety valve is which type of lever?

  1. First type, fulcrum between load and effort
  2. Third type, effort between load and fulcrum
  3. Compound lever made of two first-type levers
  4. Second type, load between fulcrum and effort
The steam force on the valve (load) acts between the fulcrum and the dead weight (effort), so R = F − P ().

Exam traps

  • Forgetting the fulcrum reaction is a vector sum for a bell crank.
  • Taking the bending arm to the lever end instead of the boss (or the fulcrum axis, if the question says so).
  • Skipping the check at the pin-hole section. The hole weakens the boss.
