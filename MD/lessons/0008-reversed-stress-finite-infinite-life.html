Reversed stress: design for finite and infinite life

About 25 minutes · Bhandari §5.10–5.11

Your win: under completely reversed stress you can (a) size a part for infinite life, (b) find the fatigue strength for a given number of cycles, (c) find the life at a given stress, and (d) combine several stress levels with Miner's rule. These are question bank Q14–Q17.
Warm-up (Lessons 6–7)
Kf from Kt and q?
\(K_f = 1 + q(K_t - 1)\), and \(K_d = 1/K_f\).
Endurance limit for axial load in terms of Se?
0.8 Se
Ductile part, static load, with a hole: use Kt?
No. Local yielding redistributes the stress.

1. Infinite life: keep the amplitude under Se

Eqs 5.31, 5.32 \[ \sigma_a = \frac{S_e}{(fs)} \qquad \tau_a = \frac{S_{se}}{(fs)} \]

That is the whole design equation. All the work is in getting Se right (Lessons 6–7).

2. Finite life: the S–N line from 10³ to 10⁶ cycles

Bhandari draws a straight line on log–log axes from point A (10³ cycles, 0.9 Sut) to point B (10⁶ cycles, Se), using the corrected Se ().

Straight line through A(3, log 0.9Sut) and B(6, log Se) \[ \log S_f = \log(0.9S_{ut}) - \frac{\log(0.9S_{ut}) - \log S_e}{3}\,(\log N - 3) \] \[ \log N = 3 + \frac{3\,[\log(0.9S_{ut}) - \log \sigma]}{\log(0.9S_{ut}) - \log S_e} \]

Preloaded with Example 5.7 (σ = 250 N/mm² on a bar with Se = 100.64). Read the life, then change N to 90 000 to see the fatigue strength.

3. Cumulative damage: Miner's rule

Each cycle at stress σi uses up 1/Ni of the life. Failure comes when the fractions add up to 1 ():

Miner's equation (Eqs 5.33, 5.35) \[ \frac{n_1}{N_1} + \frac{n_2}{N_2} + \dots = 1 \qquad\text{or, with time fractions } \alpha_i:\quad \frac{1}{N} = \frac{\alpha_1}{N_1} + \frac{\alpha_2}{N_2} + \dots \]

4. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 5.7, question bank Q15)

Example 5.7 (). A forged steel bar, 50 mm diameter, steel 40C8 (Sut = 600 N/mm²), carries a reversed bending stress of 250 N/mm². Reliability 90%. Ignore stress concentration. Find the life.
  1. \(S_e = K_aK_bK_c(0.5S_{ut}) = 0.44(0.85)(0.897)(300) = 100.64\) N/mm².
  2. Since 250 > Se, the life is finite. \(\log(0.9S_{ut}) = \log 540 = 2.7324\); \(\log S_e = 2.0028\); \(\log 250 = 2.3979\).
  3. \(\log N = 3 + \dfrac{3(2.7324 - 2.3979)}{2.7324 - 2.0028} = 3 + 1.3754 = 4.3754\).
  4. \(N = 10^{4.3754} \approx 23\,736\) cycles. In the exam, draw the S–N sketch with A, B and your point marked. It earns method marks.

5. Practice

Question bank Q16 (Ex. 5.6, ): a rotating bar of steel 45C8 (Sut = 630 N/mm²) has a corrected endurance limit of 315 N/mm². Find its fatigue strength for a life of 90 000 cycles.

\(\log 567 = 2.7536\), \(\log 315 = 2.4983\), \(\log 90\,000 = 4.9542\).

\(\log S_f = 2.7536 - \frac{2.7536-2.4983}{3}(4.9542-3) = 2.5873\Rightarrow S_f = 386.6\) N/mm².

Question bank Q17 (Ex. 5.8): at the critical fillet section of a rotating shaft, d = 30 mm and Mb = 642.9 N·m. Machined 30C8 (Sut = 500), Ka = 0.79, Kb = 0.85, Kc = 0.897, Kt = 1.72, q = 0.78. Find the life.

\(\sigma = 32(642.9\times10^3)/\pi(30)^3 = 242.5\) N/mm². \(K_f = 1 + 0.78(0.72) = 1.5616\), \(K_d = 0.640\).

\(S_e = 0.79(0.85)(0.897)(0.640)(250) = 96.4\) N/mm². \(\log N = 3 + \frac{3(\log450 - \log242.5)}{\log450 - \log96.4} = 4.203\Rightarrow N \approx 15\,960\) cycles.

Ex. 5.10: a cantilever of cold-drawn steel 20C8 (Sut = 540, Ka = 0.78) carries a completely reversed ±1000 N load 150 mm from the critical fillet section, where Kt = 1.35 and q = 0.85. Reliability 90%, Kb = 0.85. Find d for a life of 10 000 cycles. (No separate (fs): design at the fatigue strength.)

\(K_f = 1 + 0.85(0.35) = 1.2975\). \(S_e = 0.78(0.85)(0.897)(1/1.2975)(270) = 123.8\) N/mm².

\(\log S_f = \log486 - \frac{\log486-\log123.8}{3}(4-3) = 2.4886\Rightarrow S_f = 308.0\) N/mm².

\(308.0 = \dfrac{32(1000\times150)}{\pi d^3}\Rightarrow d = 17.05\) mm.

Ex. 5.11 (Miner): a part of 50C4 (Sut = 660, corrected Se = 280 N/mm²) spends 85% of its cycles at ±350, 12% at ±400 and 3% at ±500 N/mm². Find its life.

From the S–N line: N₁ = 128 798, N₂ = 37 770, N₃ = 4865 cycles.

\(\frac1N = \frac{0.85}{128\,798} + \frac{0.12}{37\,770} + \frac{0.03}{4865}\Rightarrow N \approx 62\,723\) cycles. The 3% of cycles at ±500 does almost as much damage as the 85% at ±350.

Ex. 5.5 (infinite life): a machined plate of 45C8 (Sut = 630, Ka = 0.76) narrows from 100 mm to 50 mm through 5 mm fillets (Kt = 2.27, q = 0.8). It carries a completely reversed ±50 kN axial force. Reliability 90%, Kb = 0.85, (fs) = 2. Find t.

\(K_f = 1 + 0.8(1.27) = 2.016\). \(S_e = 0.76(0.85)(0.897)(0.496)(315) = 90.54\). Axial: \(0.8 S_e = 72.43\). Permissible: 36.22 N/mm².

\(t = 50\,000/(50 \times 36.22) = 27.61\) mm.

In Bhandari's finite-life S–N line, what are the two anchor points?

  1. Sut at one cycle and Se at 10⁶ cycles
  2. Syt at 10³ cycles and S′e at 10⁶ cycles
  3. 0.9 Sut at 10⁶ and Se at 10⁸ cycles
  4. 0.9 Sut at 10³ and corrected Se at 10⁶ cycles
A = (10³, 0.9 Sut) and B = (10⁶, Se) on log–log axes, with the corrected Se (Fig. 5.27).

A part sees a reversed stress below its corrected Se. What is its predicted life?

  1. Exactly 10⁶ cycles, then failure
  2. Infinite, for a steel component
  3. Found from Miner's equation directly
  4. Between 10³ and 10⁶ cycles always
For steels the S–N curve is flat beyond 10⁶ cycles, so a stress amplitude below Se gives infinite life. (Aluminium alloys have no true endurance limit.)

Exam traps

  • Using S′e (specimen) instead of the corrected Se at point B.
  • Keeping too few decimals in the logs. Carry four.
  • Applying (fs) twice in finite-life problems. Bhandari's finite-life examples compare stress directly with Sf. Apply (fs) only if the question gives one.
