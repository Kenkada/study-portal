Unit 2 · Lesson 17

Welded joints under eccentric load, bending and torsion

About 30 minutes · Bhandari §8.12–8.14

Your win: you can size the welds of a bracket whose load is offset, either in the plane of the welds (twisting) or out of it (bending), and of a shaft welded all round under torsion. This covers question bank Q26–Q35.
Warm-up (Lessons 15, 16)
For an eccentric rivet group, how is the secondary force on each rivet found?
P″ = C·r with C = Pe/Σr², perpendicular to r.
Throat of a fillet weld?
t = 0.707h

1. Two kinds of eccentricity

Load in the plane of the welds (twisting)Load perpendicular to the plane (bending)
Primary\(\tau_1 = P/A\), parallel to P\(\tau_1 = P/A\)
Secondary\(\tau_2 = \dfrac{Mr}{J}\), ⊥ to r from G (like rivets)\(\sigma_b = \dfrac{M_by}{I}\), normal to the throat
Combinevector sum at the farthest point\(\tau_{max} = \sqrt{(\sigma_b/2)^2 + \tau_1^2}\) (principal shear)

Treat each weld as a line of throat width t. Work in terms of t, then set τ = permissible to get t, and h = t/0.707.

Section properties of weld lines (Eqs 8.23–8.26, 8.30, 8.31)

Polar, one straight weld of length l, centre at r₁ from G: \(J = A\left(\frac{l^2}{12} + r_1^2\right)\), A = lt. Add the welds: J = J₁ + J₂ + …

Bending, two welds of length b at ±d/2 (parallel to the neutral axis): \(I = \dfrac{bd^2t}{2}\). Vertical weld of length d through the axis: \(I = td^3/12\).

Circular weld, radius r: \(I = \pi t r^3\), \(J = 2\pi t r^3\). Under torsion: \(\tau = \dfrac{M_t}{2\pi t r^2}\).

2. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 8.12, bending)

Example 8.12 (). A bracket is welded to a column by two horizontal fillet welds, each 50 mm long, one at the top and one at the bottom, 100 mm apart. A 10 kN load acts 100 mm from the weld plane. τ = 70 N/mm². Find the weld size.
  1. A = 2(50t) = 100t, so τ₁ = 10 000/100t = 100/t.
  2. I = 2 × (50t)(50²) = 250 000t mm⁴ (each weld 50 mm from the neutral axis).
  3. σb = M y/I = (10 000 × 100)(50)/(250 000t) = 200/t.
  4. τmax = √((200/2t)² + (100/t)²) = 141.42/t = 70 ⇒ t = 2.02 mm, h = 2.86 → 3 mm.

3. Practice

Ex. 8.9 (in-plane): two horizontal welds, each 50 mm long, at 25 mm above and below G and centred on it horizontally. A 7.5 kN vertical load acts 125 mm from G. τ = 100 N/mm². Find the throat t. (Farthest point r = √(25² + 25²) = 35.36 mm.)

A = 100t ⇒ τ₁ = 75/t (vertical). J = 2 × 50t(50²/12 + 25²) = 83 334t. τ₂ = (7500 × 125)(35.36)/83 334t = 397.8/t at 45°.

Components: vertical 75/t + 281.3/t = 356.3/t; horizontal 281.3/t. Resultant = 454/t = 100 ⇒ t = 4.54 mm (h = 6.4 mm).

Ex. 8.13 (bending): a bracket is welded by two vertical welds, each 400 mm long. P = 50 kN at 300 mm from the weld plane. τ = 70 N/mm². Find the leg h.

A = 800t ⇒ τ₁ = 62.5/t. I = 2(t × 400³/12) = 10.67 × 10⁶ t. σb = (50 000 × 300)(200)/(10.67 × 10⁶ t) = 281.2/t.

τmax = √((140.6)² + 62.5²)/t = 153.9/t = 70 ⇒ t = 2.2, h = 3.11 → 4 mm.

Ex. 8.15 (bending, circular): a 50 mm diameter beam is welded all round to a support. P = 10 kN at 200 mm from the weld. τ = 100 N/mm². Find h.

τ₁ = 10 000/(π × 50 × t) = 63.66/t. I = πtr³ = πt(25)³. σb = (10 000 × 200)(25)/(πt × 25³) = 1018.6/t.

τmax = √(509.3² + 63.66²)/t = 513.3/t = 100 ⇒ t = 5.13, h = 7.26 → 8 mm.

Question bank Q35 / Problem 8.7 (torsion): a 75 mm shaft is welded all round to a support and carries 3000 N·m of torque. τ = 70 N/mm². Find h.

τ = Mt/(2πtr²) ⇒ 70 = 3000 × 10³/(2πt × 37.5²) ⇒ t = 4.85 mm, h = 6.86 mm (book: 6.86).

Ex. 8.11 (in-plane, three welds): two vertical welds of 150 mm, 100 mm apart, plus a 100 mm horizontal weld across their tops. G is 56.25 mm below the top weld, midway between the verticals. P = 25 kN vertical at e = 150 mm from G; τ = 55. You are given J = 1 817 708t and rmax = 106.25 mm at θ = 28.07° to the horizontal. Find h.

τ₁ = 25 000/400t = 62.5/t (vertical). τ₂ = (25 000 × 150)(106.25)/(1 817 708t) = 219.2/t; components: vertical 103.1/t, horizontal 193.4/t.

Resultant = √(165.6² + 193.4²)/t = 254.7/t = 55 ⇒ t = 4.63, h = 6.55 → 7 mm.

Question bank Q32–Q34 are Bhandari Problems 8.3–8.6 (figures on ). Book answers: 8.3 → 40 and 80 mm; 8.4 → 8.59 mm; 8.5 → 12.94 mm; 8.6 → 6.4 mm. Solve them with the figures open.

In the bending case, why is τmax = √((σb/2)² + τ₁²)?

  1. The bending stress is always half the shear stress
  2. It is the distortion energy equivalent stress
  3. It is the vector sum of two shear stresses
  4. It is the principal shear stress from σ and τ
The throat carries a normal stress σb plus a shear τ₁. The principal (maximum) shear stress is √((σ/2)² + τ²) (Lesson 2). That is compared with the weld's permissible shear stress.

For a straight weld line of length l whose centre is r₁ from G, what is J about G?

  1. A·l²/12, ignoring r₁
  2. A(l²/12 + r₁²) by the parallel-axis theorem
  3. A·r₁² only, since the weld is thin
  4. 2π t r₁³, as for a circular weld
J about the weld's own centre is ≈ Al²/12 (the thickness term is negligible). Shift to G with the parallel-axis theorem: J = A(l²/12 + r₁²) (Eq. 8.25).

Exam traps

  • Mixing up the two cases: in-plane uses J and vector addition; out-of-plane uses I and principal shear.
  • Locating G wrongly for unsymmetrical weld groups. Take moments of the weld lengths.
  • Reporting t when the question asks for size. The size is the leg, h = t/0.707.
