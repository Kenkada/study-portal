Part A: name the method

A bracket is bolted to a column; the load acts perpendicular to the bolt axes, offset from the column face. What do the bolts carry?

  1. Only direct shear, P divided by number of bolts
  2. Only tension, from tilting about the bolt centroid
  3. Direct shear plus tension from tilting about an edge
  4. Only tension, from the load divided by bolt number
Lesson 19, case 2: P′ = P/n as shear, and P″ ∝ distance from the tilting edge as tension. Combine them with maximum shear or principal stress theory.

An angle section is welded to a plate with top and bottom fillet welds and loaded through its centroid. What fixes the two weld lengths?

  1. Total length from strength; split by moments about the centroid
  2. Equal lengths, since both welds carry half the load
  3. Polar moment of inertia and vector addition of shear
  4. Principal shear from bending and direct shear stress
Lesson 16: l = P/(0.707hτ), then l₁y₁ = l₂y₂ so the weld forces have no moment about the centroid.

A fulcrum pin of a bell-crank lever is sized by:

  1. Crushing stress, then checked for bending
  2. Double shear, then checked for crushing
  3. Tension at the core, then checked for shear
  4. Bearing pressure, then checked for double shear
The lever oscillates on the pin (sliding), so a low bearing pressure governs (Lesson 21).

In a knuckle joint, the pin diameter is usually set by:

  1. Double shear, because the pin is loaded in two planes
  2. Bending of a loose pin, giving a larger diameter
  3. Crushing in the fork, because the fork is thinner
  4. Tension in the eye, at the net section of the eye
In Ex. 4.5, shear gave 28.2 mm and bending 38.8 mm. Check both, and bending usually wins (Lesson 13).

Part B: solve (all Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 4.3 (): cotter joint, d₂ = 50, d₄ = 100 mm, P = 50 kN, cotter of 30C8 (Syt = 400), (fs) = 4, b = 5t. Cotter thickness from bending?

σb = 100. \(100 = \frac{25\,000[50/4 + 50/6]}{t(5t)^2/6}\Rightarrow t = 10.77\), so 12 mm and b = 60 mm. (Shear alone gives t = 10, b = 50.)

Ex. 4.4 (): rods 50 mm, 40C8 (Syt = 380), cotter t = 15 mm, (fs) = 6. Find the spigot diameter d₂ (the rods carry their full allowable load).

σt = 63.33; P = (π/4)(50²)(63.33) = 124 348 N. (π/4)d₂² − 15d₂ = 1963.5 ⇒ d₂ = 60.45, so 65 mm. Then d₁ = 84.2 → 85; d₄ = 130.4 → 135; a = 30.2 → 35; c = 28.0 → 30 mm.

Ex. 8.20 (): Lozenge (diamond) double-strap butt joint for 250 × 20 mm tie bars, 6 rivets (1-2-3 rows), d = 6√t = 27 mm, double shear = 1.875 × single, σt = 80, τ = 60, σc = 120. Efficiency?

One rivet in double shear: 1.875(π/4)(27²)(60) = 64 412 N. Section AA (one hole): (250 − 27)(20)(80) = 356 800 N. BB: (250 − 54)(1600) + 64 412 = 378 012. CC: (250 − 81)(1600) + 3(64 412) = 463 637. All rivets: 6 × 64 412 = 386 475.

Least = 356 800; solid plate = 400 000, so η = 89.2%.

Ex. 8.21 (): boiler, Di = 1.5 m, 1.5 MPa, triple-riveted double-strap butt joint, equal straps, 5 rivets per pitch all in double shear, CA 2 mm. With t = 20, d = 27, p limited to pmax = 160 mm, find the efficiency.

Pitch from strength = 228 mm > pmax = 6(20) + 41.28 = 161.3, so p = 160.

Pt = (160 − 27)(20)(80) = 212 800; Ps = 5 × 1.875 × (π/4)(27²)(60) = 322 062; Pc = 5(27)(20)(120) = 324 000; P = 256 000. η = 83.1%.

Ex. 8.18 (): a 3 mm brake band is riveted to its hinge with four rivets in single shear; load 10 kN; σt = 80, τ = 60, σc = 120. Rivet diameter from shear?

4(π/4)d²(60) = 10 000 ⇒ d = 7.28 → 8 mm (crushing gives 6.94). Band width: (w − 2 × 8)(3)(80) = 10 000 ⇒ w = 57.7 → 60 mm.

Ex. 8.3 (): plates 120 × 12.5 mm joined by double transverse fillet welds; the weld must be as strong as the plate; σt = 110. Weld length including 15 mm?

P = 120 × 12.5 × 110 = 165 000. 1.414(12.5)(l)(110) = 165 000 ⇒ l = 84.9; +15 = 99.9, so 100 mm.

Ex. 8.8 (): an ISA 150 × 75 × 10 angle (CG 53.2 mm from the short side) is welded along its long side by two welds, 125 kN static, allowable 665 N per mm of weld. Longer weld length?

Total = 125 000/665 = 188.0 mm. l₁(150 − 53.2) = l₂(53.2) ⇒ l₁ = 66.7, l₂ = 121.3 mm.

Ex. 8.16 (): a 50 mm shaft welded all round carries 2500 N·m of torque; τ = 140. Leg size?

140 = 2500 × 10³/(2πt × 25²) ⇒ t = 4.55, h = 6.43 → 7 mm.

Ex. 7.8 (): a crane-runway bracket hangs from a roof truss on two bolts, P = 20 kN parallel to the bolts at 550 mm from the tilting edge; the bolts are at l₁ = 450 and l₂ = 50 mm; σt ≤ 75. Required area?

Direct: 10 000 N each. Tilt: P″₁ = 20 000 × 550 × 450/(450² + 50²) = 24 146. Total 34 146 N, so A = 455.3 mm² and M30 (561).

Ex. 7.13 (): a jib-crane tie-rod bracket on four bolts (2 at A, 2 at B). The 5 kN tie force at 30° to horizontal gives 625 N direct tension and 1082.5 N shear per bolt, plus 909.3 N tilting tension on the far bolts. 30C8, (fs) = 5, maximum shear stress theory. Required area?

σt = (625 + 909.3)/A = 1534.3/A; τ = 1082.5/A. τmax = √(767.2² + 1082.5²)/A = 1326.8/A = 40 ⇒ A = 33.17 mm², so M8 (36.6).

Ex. 6.7 (): a tool holder at 5 m/min, single-start Sq 48 × 8, force 500 N, collar mean radius 40 mm, μ = 0.15 everywhere. Power?

Speed: 5000/8 = 625 rpm. Thread torque 2306.6 + collar 0.15 × 500 × 40 = 3000, total 5306.6 N·mm. Power = 2π(625)(5306.6)/60×10⁶ = 0.35 kW; η = 12%.

Ex. 4.17 (): a pressure-vessel cover (D = 500 mm, 0.25 MPa) is held by a screw in compression; FeE 250, (fs) = 5. Required stressed area of the screw?

F = (π/4)(500²)(0.25) = 49 087 N; σc = 50, so a = 981.75 mm², giving M42 (1120 mm²).

Part C: theory in 5 minutes each

Answer, then open to check
Advantages of welded over riveted joints (any five).
Lighter, cheaper, leak-proof, faster, no holes so no stress concentration, better appearance, often stronger than the plates, easy to modify, can join tubes.
Compare square and trapezoidal threads for power screws.
Square: higher efficiency, no bursting pressure; but costly (single-point tool), weaker root, wear not adjustable. Trapezoidal: cheap (milled), stronger root, split nut takes up wear; but lower efficiency and side thrust.
Explain caulking and fullering.
Bevel the plate edge to 70–75° and hammer it (caulking tool: narrow; fullering tool: as wide as the plate) to close gaps and make boiler joints leak-proof. Not for plates under 6 mm.
Why a bolt of uniform strength, and two ways to make one?
To absorb shock energy across the whole bolt, not just in the threads. Turn the shank down to the core diameter, or drill an axial hole d₁ = √(d² − dc²).
Self-locking vs overhauling screw.
Self-locking: φ ≥ α, so the load won't run down (η < 50%). Overhauling: φ < α, so the load drives the screw down (useful in a Yankee screwdriver).
