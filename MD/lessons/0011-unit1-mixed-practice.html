Part A: name the method (no calculation)

A grey cast-iron C-frame carries a static 15 kN load. The section has both direct and bending stress. What governs the design?

  1. Maximum shear stress theory, with Syt
  2. Maximum principal stress, with Sut and Kt
  3. Modified Goodman diagram, with Se
  4. Distortion energy theory, ignoring Kt
Brittle material under static load: compare the largest normal stress with Sut/(fs). Any stress concentration is applied because cast iron cannot yield locally (Lessons 2, 7).

A steel shaft's torque varies 100 → 400 N·m and there is no bending. What is the design condition?

  1. Goodman line with Se and Sut
  2. Soderberg line with Se and Syt
  3. S–N line from 0.9 Sut to Se
  4. τa = Sse/(fs), then check τmax
For fluctuating torsion, Bhandari's modified Goodman diagram is horizontal at Sse: mean shear has no effect until yielding (Lesson 9).

A component must survive 50 000 cycles of reversed bending at a stress above its corrected Se. Which tool do you use?

  1. S–N line between 10³ and 10⁶ cycles
  2. Miner's rule with a single stress level
  3. Gerber parabola with zero mean stress
  4. Endurance limit divided by safety factor
Finite life under reversed stress: read the fatigue strength (or life) off the log–log line from (10³, 0.9Sut) to (10⁶, Se) (Lesson 8).

You must choose between steel and aluminium for a light, stiff square-section beam. Which index ranks them?

  1. S/ρ, strength per unit weight
  2. E/ρ, specific modulus of the tie
  3. E1/2/ρ, from beam stiffness
  4. S2/3/ρ, from beam strength
Iso-rigidity for a square beam: deflection ∝ 1/(E a⁴), so mass ∝ ρ/E1/2 (Lesson 5).

Part B: solve

Bhandari Ex. 4.12 (question bank Q11): a wall bracket of grey cast iron FG 200, (fs) = 3.5, has a rectangular section with depth 2t and width t. A 5 kN force at 60° to the vertical acts on it. At the critical section, its horizontal component has an arm of 150 mm and its vertical component an arm of 300 mm. The horizontal component also causes direct tension. Use maximum normal stress theory to find t.

Ph = 4330 N, Pv = 2500 N, M = 4330(150) + 2500(300) = 1.3995×10⁶ N·mm.

\(\sigma_b = \frac{M(t)}{t(2t)^3/12} = \frac{2.099\times10^6}{t^3}\); \(\sigma_t = \frac{4330}{2t^2} = \frac{2165}{t^2}\).

\(\sigma_b + \sigma_t = 200/3.5 = 57.14 \Rightarrow t = 33.65\) mm, so the section is 35 × 70 mm.

Bhandari Ex. 4.14 (Q12): at section XX of an overhang crank, the 1 kN crankpin force gives Mb = 175 N·m and Mt = 500 N·m. Steel 30C8 (Syt = 400), (fs) = 2. Use maximum shear stress theory to find d.

τallow = 0.5(400)/2 = 100. \(\frac{16}{\pi d^3}\sqrt{175^2+500^2}\times10^3 = 100 \Rightarrow d = 29.99\), so use 30 mm.

Bhandari Ex. 4.11: a cast-iron FG 200 cantilever ((fs) = 2.5, depth = 2 × width w) supports a pulley. The bending moment at the fixed end works out to 10 × 10⁶ N·mm. Find w.

I = w(2w)³/12 = (2/3)w⁴, y = w. \(80 = \frac{10^7(w)}{(2/3)w^4} = \frac{1.5\times10^7}{w^3}\Rightarrow w = 57.24\) mm, so the section is 60 × 120 mm.

Bhandari Ex. 5.18: same cantilever as Ex. 5.12 (Se = 126.11, Sut = 600, (Mb)a = 10 000 N·mm, tan θ = 2, (fs) = 2), but now use the Gerber criterion. Find d.

\(\frac{S_a}{126.11} + \left(\frac{S_a}{2\times600}\right)^2 = 1\Rightarrow S_a^2 + 11\,418.6S_a - 1200^2 = 0\Rightarrow S_a = 124.8\).

\(\frac{32(10\,000)}{\pi d^3} = \frac{124.8}{2}\Rightarrow d = 11.78\) mm. Slightly smaller than the Goodman result (12.13), as expected.

Bhandari Ex. 5.16: a polished steel bar, 30 mm diameter, has a groove 2 mm deep (net diameter 26 mm). Kt = 1.8, q = 0.95. Sut = 1250, S′e = 600 MPa, Kb = 0.85, reliability 90%. Axial force varies 0 → Pmax. Find the largest Pmax for 10⁵ cycles (no extra (fs)).

Kf = 1.76. \(S_e = 1(0.85)(0.897)(1/1.76)(600) = 259.8\). S–N at 10⁵ cycles: \(\log S_f = \log1125 - \frac{\log1125-\log259.8}{3}(2) \Rightarrow S_f = 423.6\).

Repeated load gives tan θ = 1. Goodman with Sf in place of Se: \(S_a = 1/(1/423.6+1/1250) = 316.4\).

\(\frac{P/2}{\frac{\pi}{4}(26)^2} = 316.4\Rightarrow P = 336\) kN.

Aluminium (ρ = 2.70, S = 200) versus steel (ρ = 7.85, S = 300). Find mAl/msteel for a tie of equal strength.

(2.70/7.85)(300/200) = 0.52.

Recall Lesson 4: a 1000 N weight falls 10 mm onto the collar of a 1 m steel bar of area 500 mm² (E = 207 000). Find the impact stress.

\(\sigma_i = \frac{W}{A}\left[1+\sqrt{1+\frac{2hAE}{Wl}}\right] = 2(1+\sqrt{2071}) = 93.0\) N/mm².

What is the second number of the R10 series (starting at 1)?

\(10^{1/10} = 1.2589\), which rounds to 1.25.

Part C: theory you should be able to write in 5 minutes

Answer aloud or on paper, then open to check
Explain the design process from need to financial viability, with an example.
Need → analysis/problem definition (specifications) → conceptual design (alternatives) → technical viability (physically realisable?) → economic viability (value > cost?) → financial viability (can it be funded?) → preliminary and detailed design. See .
List six factors that decide the factor of safety.
Effect of failure, type of load, accuracy of force analysis, material (ductile/brittle), required reliability, cost, testing possible or not, service conditions, quality of manufacture.
Distinguish low-cycle and high-cycle fatigue.
Below about 10³ cycles: low-cycle, involves local plastic yielding, designed on Sut/Syt. Above 10³: high-cycle, designed on the endurance limit.
Four methods to reduce stress concentration.
Larger fillet radius; undercut shoulder; additional (relief) notches or holes; remove unneeded material; reduce shank to the thread core diameter; drill holes beside keyways.
Why is the Goodman line preferred to Gerber in design?
It lies inside the failure points (safe), it is a straight line (simpler), and a rough sketch is enough.
