1. Why gears, and which kind

Gear drives are positive (exact, constant velocity ratio). They are compact (the smallest centre distance of any drive), carry very large power, work even at very low speed, are up to 99% efficient (spur), and allow gear shifting. Against that, they are the costliest drive, need accurate shaft alignment, and need lubrication and cleanliness ().

2. Derivation: the law of gearing (§17.5)

Prove that the velocity ratio stays constant only if the common normal at the point of contact always passes through a fixed point P on the line of centres.
  1. Gears 1 and 2 turn about O₁ and O₂ at ω₁ and ω₂ and touch at C. Velocity of C as a point of gear 1: CA = ω₁·O₁C, perpendicular to O₁C. As a point of gear 2: CB = ω₂·O₂C, perpendicular to O₂C.
  2. The teeth must neither separate nor dig in, so the components of CA and CB along the common normal NN must be equal (call it CD).
  3. Drop perpendiculars O₁G and O₂F from the centres onto NN. Similar triangles give CA/O₁C = CD/O₁G and CB/O₂C = CD/O₂F. Combining these: \(\dfrac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \dfrac{O_2F}{O_1G}\).
  4. NN cuts the line of centres at P. Triangles O₂FP and O₁GP are similar, so \(\boxed{\dfrac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \dfrac{O_2P}{O_1P}}\).
  5. O₁P + O₂P = O₁O₂ is fixed, so a constant ratio needs a fixed P, the pitch point. Only involute and cycloidal profiles satisfy this law (–650).

3. Involute or cycloidal?

Cycloidal teeth put a convex flank against a concave one, which gives more contact area and better wear strength, and they never interfere. But each tooth needs two curves (epicycloid and hypocycloid), which is hard to make accurately, and the pressure angle varies through the mesh. They survive only in clocks, watches and instruments. Involute teeth are standard because ():

  1. the law of gearing holds at any centre distance, so a small mounting error does not change the ratio;
  2. all gears of one module and pressure angle are interchangeable, and one cutter makes them all;
  3. the basic rack has straight sides, which are easy to machine accurately;
  4. the pressure angle stays constant, so the line of action is fixed.

5. Derivation: interference and the minimum number of teeth (§17.9, 17.15)

The involute starts at the base circle. If the mating tooth tip reaches below the base circle of the pinion, it meets a non-involute flank and digs in. That is interference, and it causes wear, vibration and jamming. A generating cutter automatically cuts that region away. That is undercutting, and it weakens the tooth (–658).

Derive \(z_{min} = \dfrac{2}{\sin^2\alpha}\) for a pinion meshing with a rack (addendum = m), Eq. 17.12.
  1. The line of action leaves the pitch point P at α. It touches the pinion's base circle at E, the foot of the perpendicular from the pinion centre O. Contact beyond E is non-involute, so E is the interference limit.
  2. In the right triangle OEP, OP = r = mz/2 and the angle at O is α, so PE = r sin α.
  3. The depth of E below the pitch line, measured perpendicular to it, is PE sin α = r sin²α.
  4. The rack's addendum line must not pass below E: \(m \le r\sin^2\alpha = \dfrac{mz}{2}\sin^2\alpha\Rightarrow \boxed{z \ge \dfrac{2}{\sin^2\alpha}}\).
  5. Values: 14.5° → 31.9 (32 teeth), 20° → 17.1 (17), 25° → 11.2 (11). Tip relief cuts this in practice to 27, 14 and 9. The stub system has addendum 0.8m: 1.6/sin²20° = 13.7, so 14. For 20° full depth, Bhandari's rule is to take 18 or 20 pinion teeth (–671).

Other cures for interference: a larger pressure angle (a smaller base circle), or long-and-short addendum gearing. The second makes the gears non-standard and non-interchangeable.

6. Backlash, hunting tooth, gear trains

  • Backlash is the amount by which the tooth space exceeds the mating tooth thickness on the pitch circle. It stops jamming and allows for machining errors and thermal expansion. You get it by cutting thinner teeth or by slightly increasing the centre distance. It does not change the velocity ratio ().
  • Hunting tooth: 20/41 instead of 20/40, so a given pair of teeth meets only once every 41 pinion turns. Wear evens out ().
  • Trains (§17.8). In a simple train each shaft carries one gear and the idlers do not change the ratio: an odd number of idlers keeps the direction, an even number (or none) reverses it. A compound train has two gears on one shaft, so n₁/n₄ = (z₂/z₁)(z₄/z₃). A reverted train has input and output on one axis: m₁(z₁ + z₂) = m₂(z₃ + z₄). In an epicyclic train the planets ride on a carrier around a sun gear. It is the most compact (–657).
Derive (Bhandari Ex. 17.6, ): a two-stage gearbox with identical pinions and module m, total ratio i. Show that the most compact box has i₁ = i₂ = √i.
  1. Stage 1: \(a_1 = \tfrac12 d_1'(1 + i_1)\). Stage 2, with an identical pinion (d₃′ = d₁′): \(a_2 = \tfrac12 d_1'(1 + i_2)\).
  2. The size of the box is measured by a = a₁ + a₂ = ½d₁′(2 + i₁ + i₂). Put i₂ = i/i₁: \(a = \tfrac12 d_1'\Big(2 + i_1 + \dfrac{i}{i_1}\Big)\).
  3. \(\dfrac{da}{di_1} = 0\Rightarrow 1 - \dfrac{i}{i_1^2} = 0\Rightarrow \boxed{i_1 = \sqrt i = i_2}\). For three stages the same idea gives i = ∛i′ per stage (Eq. 17.14).

7. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 17.1, also slide Q1, p. 852)

Ex. 17.1 (): a 20-tooth pinion meshes with a 120-tooth gear, m = 4 mm, 20° full depth. Find the centre distance, pitch diameters, addendum, dedendum, tooth thickness, clearance and gear ratio.
  1. a = m(zp + zg)/2 = 4(140)/2 = 280 mm.
  2. d′p = 4 × 20 = 80 mm; d′g = 4 × 120 = 480 mm.
  3. Addendum = m = 4 mm; dedendum = 1.25m = 5 mm; clearance = 0.25m = 1 mm.
  4. Tooth thickness = 1.5708 × 4 = 6.2832 mm. Gear ratio i = 120/20 = 6, the usual limit for one pair.

8. Practice (geometry steps from Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 17.5 (): two-stage box, centre distance 175 mm per stage, z₁ = 20 and z₂ = 50. Module?

m = 2a/(z₁ + z₂) = 2(175)/70 = 5 mm. Then d₁′ = 100 and d₂′ = 250 mm.

Ex. 17.11 (): motor 1000 rpm, compressor 250 rpm, centre distance exactly 250 mm, and the design gives m = 4 mm. Number of pinion teeth?

d′p + d′g = 500 and d′g/d′p = 4, so d′p = 100 and d′g = 400. zp = 100/4 = 25 and zg = 100. Fixing the centre distance fixes the diameters first, then the module sets z.

Ex. 17.12 (): two-stage reducer from 1440 rpm to about 180 rpm, identical stages, 18-tooth pinions. Choose the teeth as Bhandari does and find the actual output speed.

i′ = 1440/180 = 8, so each stage is √8 = 2.828 and zg = 2.828 × 18 = 50.9 → 51. nB = 1440 × 18/51 = 508.24 and nout = 508.24 × 18/51 = 179.4 rpm.

Ex. 17.12, gear dimensions (): m = 6 mm, pinion 18 teeth. Dedendum (root) circle diameter of the pinion?

df = m(z − 2.5) = 6(15.5) = 93 mm. Also d′ = 108 mm and da = m(z + 2) = 120 mm. For the 51-tooth gear: 306, 318 and 291 mm.

9. GATE corner

A spur gear has a module of 4 mm and 20 teeth. Its circular pitch is:

  1. 4.00 mm
  2. 6.28 mm
  3. 12.57 mm
  4. 80.0 mm
p = πm = 12.57 mm. 6.28 mm is the tooth thickness, πm/2, and 80 mm is the pitch diameter.

Minimum number of teeth on a 20° full-depth involute pinion meshing with a rack, to avoid interference (theoretical):

  1. 14
  2. 17
  3. 23
  4. 32
zmin = 2/sin²20° = 17.1. 14 is for 20° stub and 32 for 14.5° full depth.

Adding one idler gear between a driver and a driven gear:

  1. changes the speed ratio and keeps the direction
  2. changes the speed ratio and the direction
  3. keeps the direction and halves the torque
  4. keeps the speed ratio and reverses the direction
The ratio depends only on the first and last gears. One idler makes the driven gear turn the same way as the driver, which is the reverse of a direct mesh.

The fundamental law of gearing requires that the common normal at the point of contact:

  1. always passes through a fixed pitch point
  2. always passes through the base circle centre
  3. remains parallel to the line of centres
  4. remains tangent to both addendum circles
ω₁/ω₂ = O₂P/O₁P is constant only if P is fixed (§17.5).

Exam traps

  • Pitch diameter d′ = mz. The addendum circle is m(z + 2), not mz + m.
  • Gear ratio i = zg/zp is always ≥ 1. A transmission ratio for a train runs from the first driver to the last driven gear.
  • Clearance: 0.25m (20° FD), 0.157m (14.5° FD), 0.2m (stub). Learn the table.
  • Module values must be standard. Round up to Choice 1 and use Choice 2 only if a question allows it.
