Unit 3 · Lesson 31

V-belts and selecting belts from catalogues

About 30 minutes · Bhandari §13.4, 13.11–13.15

Your win: you can derive why a V-belt grips 2.92 times harder than a flat belt, find the number of V-belts from first principles, and run the standard IS/catalogue selection procedure (section, pulley sizes, pitch length, centre distance, correction factors, number of belts).
Warm-up (Lesson 30)
Flat-belt tension ratio with centrifugal tension?
(P₁ − mv²)/(P₂ − mv²) = efα
Open-belt length?
L = 2C + (π/2)(D + d) + (D − d)²/4C

1. Derivation: tension ratio of a V-belt

Derive Eq. 13.7 for a belt of included angle θ seated in a groove.
  1. The groove walls push on each side of the belt with dN/2. Their components in the radial plane add to dN sin(θ/2). The friction force on the two walls is still 2 × f(dN/2) = f dN, along the belt.
  2. Tangential: dP = f dN. Radial: dN sin(θ/2) = (P − mv²)dφ.
  3. Eliminate dN: \(\dfrac{dP}{P - mv^2} = \dfrac{f}{\sin(\theta/2)}\,d\phi\).
  4. Integrate: \(\boxed{\dfrac{P_1 - mv^2}{P_2 - mv^2} = e^{f\alpha/\sin(\theta/2)}}\). It's the flat-belt equation with an effective friction coefficient f/sin(θ/2).
  5. For θ = 40°: 1/sin 20° = 2.92. Same materials, 2.92× the friction, hence much more power and much less slip ().

2. Construction and standard sections

A V-belt has polyester cords at the neutral axis (they carry the load, with little bending stress), rubber tension and compression layers, and a cover. Belt angle 40°; pulley groove 34°–38°, which gives the wedge action. Sections Z, A, B, C, D, E (IS 2494), designated e.g. B 4430 Lp (section B, pitch length 4430 mm) ().

Disadvantages of V-belts: a large height relative to the pulley diameter raises bending stress (shorter life), lower efficiency, more creep, costlier grooved pulleys. Timing (ribbed, synchronous) belts add teeth for a positive drive with no slip, up to 80 m/s, no lubrication and little initial tension, but they cost more and are sensitive to misalignment. Used for engine camshaft drives ().

3. Selection procedure (§13.12)

Steps
  1. Service factor Fa (Table 13.15: type of driver and driven machine, hours per day). Design power = Fa × P.
  2. Section from the power–speed chart (Fig. 13.24).
  3. Smaller pulley ≥ the recommended minimum; D = ratio × d; round to preferred diameters.
  4. Pitch length from the open-belt formula, then round to a standard Lp (Table 13.14).
  5. Recalculate C from the chosen Lp (a quadratic in C).
  6. Fc (length factor, Table 13.21) and Fd (arc-of-contact factor, Table 13.22).
  7. Power rating per belt Pr = base rating + increment for the speed ratio (Tables 13.16–13.20).
  8. \(\text{Number of belts} = \dfrac{P\,F_a}{P_r\,F_c\,F_d}\), rounded up.

Belt tensioning: cut and rejoin (laced belts only); adjust the centre distance with a screw; idler pulley next to the small pulley on the slack side (increases wrap); pivoted motor (Rockwood) drive, with eccentricity e = (P₁a + P₂b)/W ().

4. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 13.8): catalogue selection

Example 13.8 (). A 7.5 kW, 1440 rpm induction motor drives a fan at about 480 rpm, 24 h per day; C ≈ 1 m.
  1. Fa = 1.3, so design power = 9.75 kW. The chart at 1440 rpm gives a B section.
  2. d = 200 mm (B minimum), D = 3 × 200 = 600 mm.
  3. L = 2000 + (π/2)(800) + 400²/4000 = 3296.6, so choose standard 3200 mm.
  4. Correct C: 3200 = 2C + 1256.6 + 40 000/C, so C = 950.6 mm.
  5. α = 180 − 2 sin⁻¹(400/1901.3) = 155.7°, so Fd ≈ 0.94; Fc (B, 3200) = 1.08.
  6. Pr (B, 200 mm, 1440 rpm, ratio 3) = 5.90 + 0.46 = 6.36 kW.
  7. Belts = 7.5 × 1.3/(6.36 × 1.08 × 0.94) = 1.51, so 2 belts of B 3200 Lp.

5. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 13.2 (): V-belt, motor pulley 300 mm at 1440 rpm, compressor pulley 900 mm, C = 1 m, μ = 0.2, θ = 40°, belt area 236.67 mm², ρ = 0.97 g/cc, allowable tension 850 N per belt. Power per belt?

α = 145.1° = 2.53 rad; e0.2(2.53)/sin20° = 4.40. m = 0.23 kg/m; v = 22.62 m/s; mv² = 117.7.

P₂ = (850 − 117.7)/4.40 + 117.7 = 284.1 N. kW = (850 − 284.1)(22.62)/1000 = 12.8. For 20 kW: 1.56, so 2 belts.

Ex. 13.7 (): pulleys 200/600 mm, groove 34°, m = 0.5 kg/m, Pmax = 500 N per belt, μ = 0.2, wrap 157°, 1440 rpm, 10 kW. Number of belts?

Effective exponent = 0.2(2.740)/sin17° = 1.874, so ratio 6.52. v = 15.08, mv² = 113.7. P₁ − P₂ = 663.1.

P₁ − 113.7 = 6.52(P₂ − 113.7) ⇒ P₁ = 897 N, P₂ = 234 N. Belts = 897/500 = 1.79, so 2.

Ex. 13.9 (): 15 kW, 2880 rpm AC motor to a centrifugal pump at about 2400 rpm, 18 h per day, C ≈ 400 mm, d = 125 mm. Given Fa = 1.2, B section, D = 150, standard Lp = 1210 (Fc = 0.87), wrap 177° (Fd = 0.99), Pr = 3.96 + 0.50. Number of belts?

15 × 1.2/(4.46 × 0.87 × 0.99) = 4.69, so 5 belts. (Corrected C = 388.8 mm.)

Ex. 13.10 (): 5 kW, 1440 rpm motor to a compressor at 970 rpm, 24 h per day, C ≈ 500, d = 150 (Fa = 1.2, B section, D = 224, Lp = 1560 with Fc = 0.92, Fd = 0.98, Pr = 3.83 + 0.36). Number of belts?

5 × 1.2/(4.19 × 0.92 × 0.98) = 1.59, so 2 belts. (C = 484.9 mm.)

6. GATE corner

A V-belt with a 40° groove and μ = 0.25 behaves like a flat belt with an effective coefficient of friction of about:

  1. 0.25 × sin 20° ≈ 0.086
  2. 0.25 / sin 20° ≈ 0.731
  3. 0.25 / cos 20° ≈ 0.266
  4. 0.25 × 2 ≈ 0.500
μeff = μ/sin(θ/2) = 0.25/0.342 = 0.731.

Why is the V-groove angle on the pulley (34°–38°) made smaller than the belt angle (40°)?

  1. To let the belt bottom out in the groove
  2. To reduce the bending stress in the belt
  3. To compensate for wear of the pulley walls
  4. To keep the wedge action as the belt bends
As the belt bends round the pulley, its section distorts and its angle reduces. A slightly smaller groove keeps it wedged on the sides, never resting on the bottom ().

Which drive gives an exact speed ratio with no lubrication?

  1. Timing (synchronous) belt
  2. V-belt with an idler
  3. Crossed flat belt
  4. Roller chain drive
Toothed belts are positive drives (no slip) and need no lubrication. Chains are positive too but need lubrication. Friction belts creep and slip.

Exam traps

  • Dividing by sin θ instead of sin(θ/2).
  • Forgetting to recompute C after choosing a standard pitch length.
  • Multiplying by Fc and Fd instead of dividing: they reduce what each belt can carry.
