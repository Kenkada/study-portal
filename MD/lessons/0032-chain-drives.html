Unit 3 · Lesson 32

Roller chain drives

About 30 minutes · Bhandari Ch. 14

Your win: you can derive the sprocket pitch diameter and the polygonal (chordal) speed variation, select a roller chain from power ratings, and find the number of links and the exact centre distance. You'll also know why a sprocket has an odd number of teeth and a chain an even number of links.
Warm-up (Lessons 30–31)
Velocity ratio limits: flat belt, V-belt, chain?
About 4:1, 7:1, and up to 10–15:1 (Bhandari recommends < 6–7:1 per stage for chains).
Which side is kept on top in a horizontal belt drive?
The slack side (for chains it's the opposite: see below).

1. Chains: features and applications

A chain is a series of links joined by pins, running on toothed sprockets. It sits between belts and gears (). Advantages: wide range of centre distances, several shafts from one sprocket, more compact than belts, no slip (positive), efficiency 96–98%, no initial tension (light bearing loads), unaffected by temperature. Disadvantages: wear at the joints stretches the pitch, parallel shafts only, polygonal speed variation, needs a housing, lubrication and tensioning, noisy. Applications: bicycles, motorcycles, automobiles, agricultural machinery, oil rigs, conveyors and material handling. Typical limits: ratio < 10:1, v ≤ 25 m/s, P ≤ 100 kW.

Types: load-lifting (link chain: hoists, winches), hauling (block chain: conveyors), power transmission (roller chain). A roller chain has pin, bushing, roller, inner and outer link plates. The rollers roll on the teeth, which cuts friction and wear. Designation: 08B = pitch 8/16 inch = 12.7 mm, British series; 16A-2 = 25.4 mm pitch, ANSI series, duplex ().

2. Derivations: geometry and polygonal effect

Derive the pitch circle diameter, the chordal speed variation, and the number of links (Eqs 14.1–14.7).
  1. Pitch diameter. Each link is a chord of the pitch circle subtending α = 360°/z. Half-chord over radius: \(\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{p/2}{D/2}\Rightarrow \boxed{D = \dfrac{p}{\sin(180°/z)}}\).
  2. Polygonal effect. The chain wraps a polygon, not a circle. When a link sits with its pins on the pitch circle it is at radius D/2 (vmax = πDn/60 000). Half a tooth later it lies flat at (D/2)cos(α/2), so vmin = vmax cos(180°/z).
  3. Fractional speed variation: \(\dfrac{v_{max} - v_{min}}{v_{max}} = 1 - \cos\dfrac{180°}{z}\): 4.1% for z = 11, 1.7% for z = 17, 0.86% for z = 24. Hence at least 17 teeth (19–21 for low noise) on the driving sprocket ().
  4. Average speed and ratio: v = z p n/60 000 (one pitch per tooth per revolution); i = n₁/n₂ = z₂/z₁.
  5. Number of links (by analogy with belt length, in units of pitch): \(L_n = \dfrac{2a}{p} + \dfrac{z_1+z_2}{2} + \left(\dfrac{z_2 - z_1}{2\pi}\right)^2\dfrac{p}{a}\). Round to an even number, then back-solve the centre distance: \(a = \dfrac{p}{4}\left[m + \sqrt{m^2 - 8\left(\dfrac{z_2-z_1}{2\pi}\right)^2}\right]\), where \(m = L_n - \dfrac{z_1+z_2}{2}\). Reduce a by 0.2–0.4% for sag.

3. Selection and design rules

Power rating (Eq. 14.8) \[ \text{required rating (Table 14.2)} = \frac{\text{kW} \times K_s}{K_1 \times K_2} \]

Ks service factor (1.0 smooth to 1.7 heavy shock with an IC engine); K₁ multiple-strand factor (1, 1.7, 2.5, 3.3, 3.9, 4.6 for 1–6 strands); K₂ tooth-correction factor (1.00 at 17 teeth, 1.11 at 19, 1.26 at 21, 1.29 at 23 … ). Table 14.2 ratings assume 17 teeth and a single strand. Ratings are based on wear, fatigue of link plates, roller impact, galling ().

  • Odd teeth, even links: every link meets every tooth in turn, so wear is spread evenly. An odd number of links would need a weak offset link.
  • Centre distance 30p to 50p; wrap on the small sprocket ≥ 120°; ratio ≤ 7:1 per stage; z₂ ≤ 100–120 (a worn chain rides out on large sprockets).
  • Horizontal layout with the tight side on top (the opposite of belts), so the slack strand doesn't foul the teeth or the other strand.
  • Lubrication: Type A (manual or drip), B (bath or disc), C (pump stream). Life about 15 000 h. Tension by moving a shaft or with an idler on the slack side.

4. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 14.4)

Example 14.4 (). 12 kW, 1400 rpm motor to a centrifugal pump at 700 rpm, moderate shock.
  1. z₁ = 17 (K₂ = 1), single strand (K₁ = 1), Ks = 1.3: required rating = 12 × 1.3 = 15.6 kW.
  2. Table 14.2 at 1400 rpm: 12B gives 18.15 kW ✓. p = 19.05 mm, d₁ = 12.07, b₁ = 11.68 mm.
  3. D₁ = 19.05/sin(180°/17) = 103.67 mm; z₂ = 34, so D₂ = 206.46 mm.
  4. a ≈ 40p = 762 mm gives Ln = 2(40) + 25.5 + (17/2π)²(1/40) = 105.7, so 106 links.
  5. m = 106 − 25.5 = 80.5 ⇒ a = 765.0 mm; with sag allowance, 0.998a = 763.5 mm.

5. Practice (Bhandari Examples)

Ex. 14.1 (): single-strand 12A chain (p = 19.05 mm, breaking load 31 100 N), 17-tooth driver at 1000 rpm, rated 14.32 kW. Factor of safety at rated power?

v = 17 × 19.05 × 1000/60 000 = 5.40 m/s; P₁ = 14 320/5.40 = 2652 N; (fs) = 31 100/2652 = 11.73.

Ex. 14.2 (): 30 kW engine, 90% efficiency to the driving shaft, 17-tooth sprocket at 300 rpm, moderate shock (Ks = 1.4), four-strand chain (K₁ = 3.3). Required chain rating?

27 × 1.4/(3.3 × 1) = 11.45 kW, so chain 16A (11.63 kW at 300 rpm).

Ex. 14.3 (): 5 kW, 1400 rpm motor to a drilling machine, ratio 3:1, a ≈ 500 mm; z₁ = 21, chain 08A (p = 12.7). With Ln = 122 links, the exact centre distance?

Required rating 5 × 1.3/1.26 = 5.16 kW, so 08A (5.28 kW). z₂ = 63. Ln = 78.74 + 42 + 1.13 = 121.9, so 122.

m = 122 − 42 = 80: a = (12.7/4)[80 + √(80² − 8(42/2π)²)] = 500.8 mm.

Ex. 14.6 (), Part I: 10 kW, 900 rpm petrol engine to a conveyor at about 235 rpm; z₁ = 21, chain 12A (p = 19.05), z₂ = 80, 132 links. Centre distance (without sag correction)?

m = 132 − 50.5 = 81.5: a = (19.05/4)[81.5 + √(81.5² − 8(59/2π)²)] = 755.1 mm. The four-strand alternative (08A, z = 25/96, 124 links) gives a = 375.8 mm, a much more compact drive.

6. GATE corner

A roller chain of pitch 15.875 mm runs on a 20-tooth sprocket. The pitch circle diameter is about:

  1. 101.1 mm, from p z / π
  2. 100.5 mm, from p / tan(9°)
  3. 101.5 mm, from p / sin(9°)
  4. 317.5 mm, from p × z
D = p/sin(180°/z) = 15.875/sin 9° = 15.875/0.1564 = 101.5 mm. (pz/π = 101.1 is the circle whose circumference equals the polygon perimeter, not the pitch circle.)

The polygonal effect in a chain drive is reduced by:

  1. increasing the number of teeth on the sprocket
  2. increasing the chain pitch for a given sprocket
  3. increasing the speed of the driving sprocket
  4. keeping the slack side of the chain on top
Speed variation = 1 − cos(180°/z), which falls as z rises. Smaller pitch (for the same sprocket diameter) means more teeth, which also helps.

Why should the number of chain links be even?

  1. An even number reduces the polygonal effect
  2. Sprockets are always made with even teeth
  3. Even links make the chain run more quietly
  4. Inner and outer links alternate in pairs
The chain is made of alternating inner and outer links. An odd count needs a weaker offset link. The sprocket has odd teeth so wear is spread evenly.

Exam traps

  • Rounding Ln to an odd number.
  • Forgetting to divide by K₁ and K₂ (or multiplying by them) when finding the required rating.
  • Using D = pz/π for the sprocket. Use p/sin(180°/z).
