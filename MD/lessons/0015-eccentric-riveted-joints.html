Unit 2 · Lesson 15

Eccentrically loaded riveted joints

About 25 minutes · Bhandari §8.29 (same method as bolts in shear, §7.11)

Your win: when a bracket's load misses the centre of a rivet group, you can split it into primary (direct) and secondary (twisting) shear, add them as vectors, find the worst rivet, and size it. This covers Tutorial Q10 and question bank Q36–Q39, and the identical bolt problems in Lesson 19.
Warm-up (Lessons 1, 14)
Moving a force P sideways by distance e: what must you add?
A couple P·e (Lesson 1, eccentric loading).
Lap-joint rivets: single or double shear?
Single shear.

1. The idea in two steps

Move P to the centre of gravity G of the rivet group and add the couple P·e. The direct force is shared equally. The couple is resisted by forces proportional to each rivet's distance r from G, at right angles to r ().

Eqs 8.63–8.67 \[ P' = \frac{P}{n}\ \text{(primary, parallel to P)} \qquad P''_i = C\,r_i,\ \ C = \frac{Pe}{\sum r_i^2}\ \text{(secondary, } \perp r_i) \] \[ P_i = \vec P' + \vec P''_i \qquad P_{max} = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2\,\tau \]

Which rivet is worst? The one farthest from G, and where P′ and P″ point most nearly the same way. Sketch both arrows on every candidate before calculating. When the angle between them is θ: \(P_i = \sqrt{P'^2 + P''^2 + 2P'P''\cos\theta}\).

2. Worked example (Bhandari Ex. 8.24, question bank Q37)

Example 8.24. A bracket is riveted to a column with four identical rivets in a vertical line, 100 mm apart. Load 25 kN at e = 100 mm from the rivet line. τ = 60 N/mm². Find d.
  1. G is midway between rivets 2 and 3: r₁ = r₄ = 150 mm, r₂ = r₃ = 50 mm.
  2. Primary: P′ = 25 000/4 = 6250 N each, downwards.
  3. \(C = \dfrac{25\,000(100)}{2(150^2) + 2(50^2)} = 50\) N/mm. P″₁ = P″₄ = 7500 N; P″₂ = P″₃ = 2500 N.
  4. Rivets 1 and 4 are worst; P′ ⊥ P″, so P₁ = √(6250² + 7500²) = 9763 N.
  5. \(\frac{\pi}{4}d^2(60) = 9763\Rightarrow d = 14.4\), so 15 mm.

3. Practice

Ex. 8.25: two rivets 100 mm apart. The 15 kN load acts along the line of the rivets, 50 mm from G (so e = 50 mm), in a direction that makes P′ and P″ add at one rivet. τ = 60 N/mm². Find d.

P′ = 7500 N. C = 15 000(50)/(50² + 50²) = 150, P″ = 150 × 50 = 7500 N. At rivet 1 they are collinear and additive: P₁ = 15 000 N.

(π/4)d²(60) = 15 000 ⇒ d = 17.84 mm, so 18 mm.

Ex. 8.26: four rivets on a circle of radius 100 mm about G. 5 kN load at e = 200 mm. τ = 60 N/mm². At the worst rivet, P′ and P″ are collinear. Find d.

P′ = 1250; C = 5000(200)/(4 × 100²) = 25; P″ = 2500. Worst: 1250 + 2500 = 3750 N.

d = √(4 × 3750/(π × 60)) = 8.92 mm.

Ex. 8.27: nine rivets in a 3 × 3 square grid, 100 mm pitch, G at the centre rivet. 50 kN at e = 300 mm. Corner rivets on the load side have P″ at 45° to P′. Find the maximum rivet force.

P′ = 50 000/9 = 5555.6. Σr² = 4(100²) + 4(141.42²) = 120 000, so C = 50 000(300)/120 000 = 125. Corner P″ = 125 × 141.42 = 17 677.5.

\(P_3 = \sqrt{(17\,677.5\sin45°)^2 + (17\,677.5\cos45° + 5555.6)^2} = 21\,960\) N ≈ 21.96 kN, giving d = 21.6 → 22 mm.

Tutorial Q10 / Bhandari Problem 8.16 (open Fig. 8.81 on ): six identical rivets, 60 kN at 200 mm from the column centre, τ = 150 N/mm². The book gives the maximum force as 35.38 kN on rivet 2 or 4. Find d.

(π/4)d²(150) = 35 380 ⇒ d = 17.33 mm. Work out the 35.38 kN yourself from the figure: P′ = 10 kN, then C from Σr², then vector addition at rivets 2 and 4.

How is the secondary shear force on a rivet directed?

  1. Parallel to the applied load P
  2. Along the line from G to the rivet
  3. Perpendicular to the line from G to the rivet
  4. Towards the point where P is applied
The couple tries to rotate the bracket about G. Each rivet resists with a force tangential to its circle about G, i.e. perpendicular to r, with magnitude ∝ r.

Doubling the eccentricity e while keeping P the same affects:

  1. The secondary forces only, which double
  2. The primary forces only, which double
  3. Both forces, which each double
  4. Neither force, since P is unchanged
P′ = P/n doesn't depend on e. P″ = Per/Σr² is proportional to e.

Exam traps

  • Measuring e from the column face or the load to the wrong point. e is from G of the rivet group to the line of action of P.
  • Adding P′ and P″ as plain numbers when they are at an angle. Add them as vectors.
  • Checking only the farthest rivet. A nearer rivet where the arrows align can be worse (Ex. 8.27: corner rivet vs middle rivet).
