End-sem Nov 2025, Q3(b)

5 marksCompare and contrast Plasma Arc Machining (PAM), Electron Beam Machining (EBM) and Ion Beam Machining (IBM) in terms of principles, parameters and applications. Discuss their suitability for micro- and nano-scale machining.

Suitability. PAM is unsuitable for micro or nano work: wide kerf, mm-level tolerance and a large HAZ make it a macro cutting process. EBM is well suited to micro-machining: fine, electronically steerable spot, holes of tens of µm, no tool wear. Its recast/HAZ and vacuum batches limit nano work, although EB lithography reaches sub-micron patterns. IBM is the best for nano-scale machining: atom-by-atom removal, nm depth control, no HAZ, works on any material. It is too slow for anything larger.

A method for choosing

  1. Is the work electrically conductive? No → EDM, Wire EDM and transferred-arc PAM are out. Think LBM, EBM, IBM, or Unit 1 processes (USM, AJM, AWJM).
  2. Is any heat damage acceptable? No → leave the thermal family: ECM (conductive) or AWJM/USM (non-conductive). Heat-sensitive composites go to AWJM.
  3. What is the feature? Blind 3D cavity → die-sink EDM. Through-contour in plate → Wire EDM. Many tiny holes in thin sheet → EBM or LBM. Thick plate profile → PAM. Nano pattern or finishing → IBM.
  4. Any material trap? Reflective, conductive Al/Cu → avoid LBM. Very large part → avoid EBM/IBM (vacuum chamber). Tight tolerance → avoid PAM.
  5. Volume and cost. Mass perforation favours EBM; one-off dies favour EDM/Wire EDM; open-air flexibility favours LBM.

Scenario drill (MSE style, 2 marks each)

Answer from memory: process + two or three reasons. Some answers come from Units 1–2 on purpose.

S1 · Tut I Q7. Part: 4 mm thick copper alloy, intricate internal contours, thermal distortion must be minimised. Which AMP?

Wire EDM. Copper alloy conducts; internal through-contours are exactly what a CNC wire cuts (start hole + profile); DI water cooling gives a very thin recast layer and no distortion from force or bulk heating. LBM is poor on reflective copper; PAM's HAZ and kerf are far too large.

S2 · Tut I Q10. High-volume production of precision fuel-injector holes in hardened steel. Justify.

EDM (micro-EDM drilling). Hardened steel conducts; EDM drills small, deep, burr-free holes with consistent size and good finish regardless of hardness, and is a standard method for injector nozzles (Jagadeesha §6.13). Mention alternatives: EBM is listed for diesel injector metering holes and is faster per hole (Jain, p.225), and LBM is faster still, but both leave more recast/HAZ and taper.

S3 · Tut I Q13. A turbine blade in Inconel needs high-precision cooling holes. Which AMP?

LBM (percussion drilling) is the most commonly used method for turbine-blade cooling holes (Jain, p.196): fast, any angle, no tool. If precision and minimal recast matter most, EDM drilling (Jain's self-test lists it for 0.5 mm holes with L/D up to 75) or ECM/STEM (Unit 2) are preferred. A strong answer names the trade-off.

S4 · Tut I Q12. Cut heat-sensitive CFRP panels. Which AMP and why?

Abrasive water jet machining (Unit 1). It is a cold process with no HAZ, so no resin burning or delamination from heat, and CFRP is non-conductive anyway. Thermal processes (LBM, PAM) would char the matrix; EDM cannot cut it.

S5 · Tut I Q14. Machine a complex ceramic component without thermal damage or tool wear.

AJM / AWJM (Unit 1): non-thermal erosion and no shaped tool to wear. Ceramics are non-conductive (no EDM/ECM). LBM and EBM cause thermal cracking. USM fits brittle ceramics too but its tool wears, which the question rules out.

S6. A fabrication shop must profile-cut 60 mm stainless-steel plates quickly. Oxy-fuel fails. Why, and what instead?

Transferred-arc PAM. Stainless steel forms a refractory oxide skin and conducts heat well, so the oxy-fuel reaction cannot sustain the cut. Plasma at 11,000–30,000 °C melts it directly at high speed; thick plate up to ~150 mm. Accept the wide kerf and HAZ, which are fine for fabrication.

S7. Ten thousand 0.3 mm holes in a 1 mm thick nickel-alloy combustor liner.

EBM. Built for mass perforation of thin sheets: very fine spot, electronic beam deflection, holes in microseconds (Jain cites thousands of <1 mm holes in combustor domes; 100× faster than EDM for a spinning head). LBM is the open-air alternative.

S8. Mill a 200 nm deep diffraction grating on silicon, or thin a specimen for an electron microscope.

IBM. Atom-by-atom sputtering gives nm depth control; no melting or HAZ; directional milling through a photoresist mask gives sharp walls; works on semiconductors and insulators. Its slowness does not matter at this scale.

S9. A tap has broken inside an expensive hardened die. How do you remove it?

EDM. A tubular or shaped electrode erodes the hardened tap without force, so the die is not damaged. A classic EDM application (Jain, p.159).

S10. A blind, intricate 3D cavity in a hardened H13 forging die.

Die-sinking EDM. A graphite or copper electrode shaped as the cavity's negative is sunk after hardening, so no heat-treatment distortion. Wire EDM cannot make blind cavities.

Interleaved quick-fire

1. Cut 80 mm thick stainless-steel plate quickly:

  1. EBM
  2. IBM
  3. PAM
  4. LBM

Thick plate and speed: transferred-arc PAM. The others are thin-section processes.

2. Pattern a 1 µm feature on a silicon wafer with no heat damage:

  1. IBM
  2. PAM
  3. EDM
  4. WEDM

Nanometre control and no HAZ: IBM.

3. Square through-profile in a carbide punch plate:

  1. LBM
  2. PAM
  3. IBM
  4. WEDM

Conductive carbide, through-contour, tight tolerance: Wire EDM.

4. Deep, accurate holes in a pure copper block:

  1. LBM
  2. EDM
  3. PAM
  4. IBM

Copper reflects lasers and conducts heat away. EDM does not care about reflectivity.

5. Machine a heat-sensitive glass-fibre composite panel:

  1. EBM
  2. LBM
  3. AWJM
  4. PAM

Non-thermal is the deciding constraint: abrasive water jet (Unit 1).

6. Perforate thousands of fine holes in thin sheet, fastest:

  1. EBM
  2. EDM
  3. WEDM
  4. IBM

Electronically deflected beam drilling holes in microseconds: EBM's signature application.
