One idea, six machines: how heat removes metal

Every Unit 3 process concentrates energy into a tiny spot until the material melts and vaporises. Only one of them, IBM, does something different.

The common chain

Each thermal process runs the same chain. An energy source is carried to the workpiece and focused onto a spot only micrometres to millimetres wide. The local temperature climbs past the melting point and often past boiling. The molten or vapour material is then thrown out by vapour pressure, by the collapse of a bubble, or by a gas jet.

Because the tool never touches the work, hardness does not matter. What matters is how the work responds to heat: its conductivity, melting point and reflectivity.

The three-question classifier
  1. What carries the energy? Spark, photons, electrons, plasma or ions.
  2. Must the work conduct electricity? Only EDM, Wire EDM and transferred-arc PAM need it.
  3. Melting or knocking out? Everything melts and vaporises except IBM, which sputters atoms by momentum transfer.
Trap

The syllabus lists IBM under "thermal energy based" processes because it is a high-energy beam process like EBM. Its removal mechanism is not thermal. Saying so explicitly in an answer shows real understanding.

What every melting process leaves behind

When metal melts and refreezes on the surface, you get a recast layer. Under it sits a heat-affected zone (HAZ) that was heated but not melted. Both carry microcracks and residual tensile stress, so fatigue strength drops. Jain reports the endurance limit of spark-eroded steel is about 15% lower than turned steel, and notes that LBM also reduces fatigue strength (Jain, p.156 and p.193).

So in any "limitations" list for EDM, Wire EDM, LBM, EBM or PAM, recast layer + HAZ is a guaranteed point. IBM is the exception: no melting means practically no HAZ.

Check yourself

Without scrolling up: list the six Unit 3 processes. For each, say whether the workpiece must conduct electricity and whether material is removed by melting or by sputtering.

  • EDM: must conduct · melting/vaporisation
  • Wire EDM: must conduct · melting/vaporisation
  • LBM: need not conduct · melting/vaporisation
  • EBM: need not conduct · mainly vaporisation
  • PAM: transferred arc must conduct, non-transferred need not · melting, blown out by jet
  • IBM: need not conduct · sputtering (momentum transfer, no melting)

1. Which Unit 3 process removes material without melting it?

  1. Plasma arc machining
  2. Ion beam machining
  3. Electron beam machining
  4. Laser beam machining

IBM removes atoms by momentum transfer (sputtering). EBM and LBM vaporise material; PAM melts it and blows it out.

2. Which set of processes needs an electrically conductive workpiece?

  1. EDM, WEDM, LBM, EBM
  2. LBM, EBM, IBM, PAM
  3. EDM, WEDM, transferred PAM
  4. All six thermal processes

The spark or the transferred arc must flow through the work. Light, electron beams and neutralised ion beams do not need a conductive target.

3. Why must EBM run in a vacuum?

  1. Electrons scatter off air molecules
  2. Vacuum cools the workpiece faster
  3. Vacuum raises the dielectric strength
  4. Air would conduct the current

Collisions with gas molecules would scatter the beam and waste its energy. The vacuum also stops the hot filament oxidising (Jain, p.223).

4. Which layer sits directly on the surface of an EDM'd part?

  1. Converted layer
  2. Parent metal
  3. Heat-affected zone
  4. Recast layer

Order from the surface down: recast (white) layer, HAZ, converted layer, unaffected parent metal. Lesson 6 has the sketch.

5. Rank PAM, EBM and IBM from finest to coarsest feature size.

  1. PAM, then EBM, then IBM
  2. EBM, then IBM, then PAM
  3. IBM, then EBM, then PAM
  4. EBM, then PAM, then IBM

IBM removes atom layers (nanometres). EBM drills holes of tens of micrometres. PAM cuts kerfs millimetres wide. This ranking is the core of End-sem 2025 Q3(b).
